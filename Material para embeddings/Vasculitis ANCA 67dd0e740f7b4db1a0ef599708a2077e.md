# Vasculitis ANCA

**Vasculitis necrotizante** → afecta casi cualquier órgano → riñón, pulmón, vía aérea superior, piel, ojos, nervios

Granulomatosis con poliangeitis y poliangeitis microscópica + EGPA

Mortalidad 2-3 veces lo normal.

**Anticuerpos anti citoplasma de neutrófilos**

Patrones en IFI en ethanol-fixed neutrophils. 

**Perinuclear** (p-ANCA), asociado a **MPO**

Difuso o **citoplasmatica** (c-ANCA) asociado a **PR3.** 

ANCAs detected by ELISA are called MPO-ANCAs or PR3-ANCAs.

ANCA no son solo MPO y PR3, como alfa enolasa, azurocidina, BPI, catepsina G, elastasa, defensiva, lactoferrina, LAMP2 y moesina. La Patogenia de estos es baja y poco importante y el ANCA esta bajo. 

10-30% de los GPA tienen ANCA negativo, 5% de las MPA, 30-60% de las EGPA.

### Etiología

**Factores genéticos**

PR3 se asocia a HLA-DP. HLA-DPB1 04

MPA se asocia a HLA-DQ, *HLA-DRB1*09:01 en Asia y Europa* 

En Asia es más frecuente MPO

Non-MHC: ganancia de función de *PTPN22 regulador negativo de IL-10* (encoding tyrosine-protein phosphatase non-receptor type 22), *SERPINA1* (encoding α1-antitrypsin), *PRTN3* (encoding PR3) and *SEMA6A* (encoding semaphorin 6a).

**Factores epigenéticos**

Trimetilación de DNA implicada en la regulación de MPO y PRTN3 se asocia a sobreexpresión de MPO y PR3.

**Factores ambientales**

Infecciosos, drogas, **silice**. 

Toxic shock syndrome toxin 1 del *Staphylococcus aureus*, riesgo de recurrencia GPA. 

**Mecanismos de producción de ANCA**

***Regulación desordenadas de las trampas extracelulares de neutrófilos*** contribuye a la formación de ANCA. Durante la formación de NET el PR3 y MPO salen mezclados con fibras de cromatina y se unen al DNA lo que cambia la antigenicidad de estos.

Los pacientes con vasculitis tienen problemas para degradar los NETS, lo que depende de DNasa I.

**Patogenia de los ANCA**

No solo son marcadores, tienen un rol patogénico. 

Infección → activación de neutrófilos y producción de citoquinas como TNF e IL-1β, los ANCA se unen a los antígenos de los neutrófilos. Al mismo tiempo, el fragmento Fc de estos ANCA se une a receptores Fcγ de neutrófilos induciendo la activación excesiva de neutrófilos. La hiperactivación resulta en la producción anormal de citoquinas acompañada por la liberación de ROS y enzimas líticas que pueden dañar las células endoteliales.

La excesiva activación de neutrófilos elicita los ANCA ye induce a producir más NET y esto daña más a las células epiteliales.

**¿Porqué no produce depósitos inmunes?**

Luego de que ANCA estimule neutrófilos y NET, la elastasa de neutrófilos digiere los ANCA.

![image.png](Vasculitis%20ANCA%2067dd0e740f7b4db1a0ef599708a2077e/image%201.png)

**Asociación de ANCA con la actividad de la enfermedad**

No necesariamente reflejan la actividad, salvo por el compromiso renal.

Esto puede ser por las diferencias en los epítopes y afinidad de los ANCA.

MPO es una glicoproteína de 158 kDa considentente en 2 cadenas pesada, 2 pesadas y 2 molécula haem. Los ANCA MPO lo reconocen en un epítope en aminoterminal de las cadenas pesadas. 

Los AntiMPO puede ser subdividos en baja afinidad y alta afinidad. La actividad de la vasculitis es más alta cuando los antiMPO tienen más afinindad. En los pacientes con menos actividad, los anti MPO pueden hacer objetivo a epitopos en áreas de bajo riesgo.

### Granulomatosis con poliangeitis

La preparación o priming de neutrófilos también pasa, similar a MPA. Pero, se forman granulomas necrotizantes en el tracto resiratorios con ANCA PR3.

***Formación de granuloma necrotizante*** 

Infecciones como s. Aureus activa **macrófagos en tejido, como el árbol bronquial vía TLR**. Macrógagos liberan citoquinas como TNF e IL-1B → recluta neutrófilos y monocitos..

***Neutrófilos*** liberan ROS y enzimas líticas y van a lisis, resultando en la formación de un centro ***necrótico***. 

PR3 de los neutrófilos apoptóticos interfieren en mecanismos antiinflamatorios seguido de fagocitosis por macrófagos.

Monocitos reclutados se hacen macrófagos y secretan IL-23 → Diferenciación a Th17 → IL-17 → formación del granuloma.

***Producción de PR3-ANCA***

Similar mecanismo a MPO. Dependería de genética. S. aureus produce consecuencias genéticas en gen PRTN3.

### EGPA

EGPA is characterized by allergic manifestations such as adult-onset asthma and sinusitis. Eosinophilia and a prominent infiltration of eosinophils into vasculitic lesions are distinct features of EGPA. About 50% of patients with EGPA are positive for MPO-ANCAs, and the presence of these autoantibodies shows a positive correlation with renal involvement but an inverse correlation with cardiac involvement[97](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR97). Interestingly, the prevalence of ANCAs in patients with EGPA decreases over time[98](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR98).

**Eosinophilia and infiltration of eosinophils**

EGPA is regarded as a TH2-cytokine-mediated disease because elevated levels of TH2 cytokines (such as IL-4, IL-5 and IL-13) are associated with eosinophilia in patients with EGPA[99](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR99). C-C motif chemokine 26 (CCL26; also known as eotaxin 3) released from vascular endothelial cells is implicated in the tissue infiltration of eosinophils[100](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR100). The tissue-infiltrating eosinophils secrete eosinophilic granules, including eosinophilic neurotoxin, major basic proteins and eosinophilic cationic proteins, resulting in tissue destruction[101](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR101). Although eosinophil peroxidase shares 68% amino acid identity with neutrophil MPO[102](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR102), the mechanism of MPO-ANCA production in patients with EGPA remains to be identified.

**Anti-lactoferrin antibody in EGPA**

Anti-lactoferrin antibodies (one of the ‘minor’ ANCAs) are present in a subgroup of patients with EGPA but not in patients with MPA or GPA[32](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR32). The frequency of renal involvement, serum C-reactive protein levels and disease activity are all considerably higher in anti-lactoferrin-antibody-positive than in anti-lactoferrin-antibody-negative patients with EGPA. Lactoferrin is present in neutrophil-specific granules and is immediately secreted (via degranulation) upon their activation. Lactoferrin is an endogenous suppressor of NET formation[103](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR103); thus, anti-lactoferrin antibodies inhibit the NET-suppressing activity of lactoferrin and consequently promote NET formation after activation of neutrophils[32](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR32). Therefore, the presence of anti-lactoferrin antibodies is thought to be associated with NET-related disease activity in patients with EGPA.

### Vasculitis ANCA por drogas

PTU, benziltiouracilo, metimazol, carbimazol, minociclina, cefotaxima, nitrofuraoidena

Many drugs, such as propylthiouracil, benzylthiouracil, methimazole, carbimazole, minocycline, cefotaxime, nitrofurantoin, d-penicillamine, hydralazine, allopurinol, levamisole, phenytoin, sulfasalazine, rifampicin, anti-TNF agents and cocaine, can induce AAV[104](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR104),[105](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR105). Although several different pathways might lead to the development of drug-induced AAV, the innate and adaptive immune responses share some common mechanisms.

Propylthiouracil treatment induces the formation of DNase-I-resistant NETs[37](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR37). Because propylthiouracil does not inhibit DNase I activity directly, metabolites of propylthiouracil are hypothesized to mask the DNase I recognition sites in DNA extruded in NETs[37](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR37). Cocaine and levamisole induce the formation of NETs enriched in neutrophil elastase and, potentially, in mitochondrial DNA, which is highly inflammatory[106](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR106). Hydralazine also increases NET formation[107](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR107), and NETs induced by these drugs or reagents could be a source of antigens leading to ANCA formation. Some drugs, such as hydralazine, induce promoter demethylation of DNA in T cells, resulting in their activation[107](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41584-018-0145-y#ref-CR107). Activated T cells contribute to autoantibody production by B cells and plasma cells.

### **Tratamiento: Inducción de remisión**

***Ciclofosfamida***

Bajó la letalidad de la enfermedad. 

VO = EV en cuanto a efectividad. EV usa menos dosis acumulada.

*CYCLOPS trial (2009) - EV vs VO en generalizado - VO y EV similares.  Se usó mantenimiento con AZA y corticoides por 18 meses.  Enfermedad recurrió 40% EV y 21% VO. Ya no se usan estos esquemas tan largos, se suele preferir **esquema de unos 3 meses.***

EV: 15 mg/Kg cada 2 semanas x 3, luego cada 3 semanas (máximo 6 meses)

VO: 2 mg/Kg/día VO hasta remisión, luego 1,5 mg/Kg/día por 3 meses.

**Avacopan**
 small molecule inhibiting the C5a receptor
(C5aR1), was approved as an adjunctive therapy for remission
induction in 2021. In the ADVOCATE trial, patients
received a mean daily glucocorticoid dose of 47 mg in the
avacopan group and 52 mg in the prednisone group in
the 2 weeks before randomisation and up to 20 mg/day
of prednisone at randomisation.77 The baseline prednisone
treatment in the avacopan group was tapered to
discontinuation within 4 weeks and the control group
received a glucocorticoid regimen that was tapered to
discontinuation by 20 weeks. Avacopan (or placebo)
treatment was continued until week 52. The primary
endpoint was defined at two time intervals (week 26 and
week 52) as remission and no glucocorticoid use in the
preceding 4 weeks. The avacopan and prednisone groups
were non-inferior to each other for efficacy at 26 weeks,
with 72% versus 70% achieving the primary endpoint,
respectively, and at week 52, 66% of the avacopan group
and 55% of the prednisone group remained in remission
(p=0·007). Rituximab was not readministered at 26 weeks
in the ADVOCATE trial, and had rituximab
readministration occurred—which is now recognised by
health authorities as standard of care78—the outcomes at
52 weeks could have been difficult to separate.
In patients with kidney disease, the estimated
glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) had increased on
average by 7·3 mL/min per 1·73 m² in the avacopan
group and 4·1 mL/min per 1·73 m² in the prednisone
group at week 52 (p=0·029).77 An analysis, including
50 patients with an eGFR of less than or equal to
20 mL/min per 1·73m² at baseline (27 patients were
randomised to avacopan and 23 to prednisone), found
that eGFR increased on average by 16·1 mL/min per
1·73 m² (avacopan) and 7·7 mL/min per 1·73 m²
(prenisone),79 further underlining the potential of
avacopan to contribute to improved kidney function
recovery.
Over 52 weeks, patients in the avacopan group received
a total exposure of 1·7 g of glucocorticoids compared
with 3·8 g in the prednisone group, corresponding to
mean daily doses of 5 mg and 13 mg, respectively.77
Avacopan is therefore more likely to have an essential
role in the management of patients with significant
kidney disease and in situations in which it is crucial to
minimise glucocorticoid exposure.
Plasma exchange
The role of plasma exchange remains debated in the
management of ANCA-associated vasculitis. Results of
the MEPEX trial, a study published in 2007, suggested
that the addition of plasma exchange reduced the risk of
end-stage kidney disease at 12 months compared with a
regimen that used intravenous methylprednisolone
pulses.80 However, the mortality rate during the first year
was high in both groups: 27% in the plasma exchange
group and 24% in the intravenous methylprednisolone
group. These high fatality rates were more often due to
infectious complications than to features of active disease
or comorbidities of vasculitis.80
The randomised (but unblinded) PEXIVAS study
included 286 patients with PR3-ANCA-associated

    positivity and detection of circulating B cells (usually by
    fluorescence-activated cell sorting analysis) within

    factors for relapse.106 Finally, a lower eGFR at baseline is

    better preserved eGFRs because of the higher risk of
    subsequent end-stage kidney disease. For example, one
    study indicated that patients with a mean serum
    creatinine concentration of 3·9 mg/dL at baseline had a

    Specific biomarkers indicating risk of disease relapse
    remain at the investigational stage and are not yet
    available for routine care. However, active glomerulonephritis
    in the setting of disease relapses has been
    linked to an increase in soluble CD163 in the urine, and
    an assay for this marker distinguished reliably between
    vasculitis activity and other causes of acute kidney
    injury.108 Persistent haematuria at 6 months was

    with ANCA-associated glomerulonephritis.109 However,
    another study with interval repeat kidney biopsies
    showed that although 60% of patients with histologically
    proven active disease had no haematuria, 59% of patients
    with inactive disease also had haematuria.110 Another
    study of 535 patients with kidney disease found no
    statistically significant predictors of renal relapse,
    showing that they can be difficult to diagnose. A high
    index of suspicion for the possibility of relapse, and close
    monitoring for proteinuria and haematuria increases are
    beneficial.107 C-reactive protein concentrations and
    erthyrocyte sedimentation rates are both generally
    elevated during relapse, especially major ones.111 Data

    Impaired vaccine effectiveness and
    hypogammaglobulinaemia
    Studies on rituximab treatment for remission maintenance
    and the evolution of the COVID-19 pandemic solidified
    concerns about continuous B-cell depletion, which has the
    potential to induce secondary immunodeficiency, decrease
    the efficacy of vaccines, and increase the risk of lifethreatening
    COVID-19 infections. Despite the absence of,
    or a reduced humoral response to vaccines in the first
    6 months after rituximab administration, the cellular
    immune response remains intact.113
    Hypogammaglobulinaemia is another potential
    concern with continuous B-cell depletion strategies. For
    patients treated with rituximab, the risk of hypogammaglobulinaemia—
    defined as a serum immunoglobulin
    G (IgG) concentration less than 7 g/L and
    associated with an increased risk of infection—might be
    greater than 40% 6 months after induction therapy with
    rituximab.114 Other risk factors include age (per 10-year
    increase) and higher doses of glucocorticoids at induction
    of remission (per 10 mg increase of prednisone). The
    concern of hypogammaglobulinaemia
    among patients
    treated with rituximab suggests that serum IgG
    concentrations should be measured before each
    rituximab administration, and patients with reduced IgG
    concentrations should be managed in close collaboration
    with an immunologist and might require IgG replacement.

    Glucocorticoid toxicity
    In the treatment of ANCA-associated vasculitis, the use of
    glucocorticoids has been considered unavoidable to
    control disease. Clinical trials published in the past 4 years
    have evaluated the safety and efficacy of novel and
    established agents to reduce cumulative exposure to
    glucocorticoids (tables 4, 5).73,77,81 For example, the
    PEXIVAS reduced-dose regimen was non-inferior to a
    standard-dose regimen in terms of efficacy, substantially
    decreased the cumulative glucocorticoid dose needed to
    control the disease, and reduced the risk of serious
    infections.81 All three major guidelines or
    recommendations acknowledge the reduced-dose
    regimen as the new standard of care.75 The ADVOCATE
    trial replaced a standard prednisone taper with avacopan
    for ANCA-associated vasculitis and marked an important
    step forward for vasculitis trials, because it used a
    standardised instrument to measure change in
    glucocorticoid toxicity.77,116 The avacopan group had lower
    Glucocorticoid Toxicity Index (GTI) scores at both
    13 weeks and 26 weeks after treatment initiation.117 In
    addition, avacopan was superior to the standard of care in
    reducing glucocorticoid toxicity at several GTI thresholds,
    including the minimum clinically important difference of
    10 points.116
    In practice settings, high glucocorticoid doses tend to
    be maintained for longer periods of time and patients are
    often left on maintenance glucocorticoid regimens.
    Thus, real-world patients are more likely to receive
    substantially more glucocorticoids than are patients in
    trials. Although patients who reached the primary
    endpoint discontinued their prednisone no later than
    21 weeks into the ADVOCATE trial,77 glucocorticoid
    toxicity continued to increase in both the avacopan group
    and the prednisone group at both GTI timepoints. More
    than 90% of all patients in the trial had glucocorticoid
    toxicity at both week 13 and week 26. These findings
    underscore the need for strategies that reduce the
    duration of glucocorticoid courses further, minimising
    glucocorticoid use but still exerting and maintaining
    disease control.
    
    Novel treatment strategies over the next few years will
    probably include tissue-adjacent B-cell subsets with
    traditional anti-CD20 approaches, such as obinutuzumab
    or newer monoclonal antibodies targeting CD19. Research
    into anti-CD19 directed CAR-T therapies for patients with
    ANCA-associated vasculitis with the greatest unmet
    medical need is now increasing rapidly. Other B celltargeted
    therapies aimed at immunomodulation rather
    than depletion, and further efforts to inhibit relevant
    components of complement pathways are also promising
    potential strategies. Competitive interference with the
    neonatal Fc receptor as a way of swiftly reducing ANCA
    titres to facilitate disease control is another appealing
    approach. There could also be improvement in both
    induction and remission, with more effective and quicker
    means of suppressing disease flares early, as well as
    approaches to remission maintenance that do not involve
    continuous B-cell depletion or long-term glucocorticoid
    use.
    Over the next 10 years, the likelihood of continued
    incremental progress for patients with ANCA-associated
    vasculitis is high. Substantial leaps forward in the ability
    to diagnose, treat, and monitor ANCA-associated
    vasculitis, however, will require breakthroughs in 
    
    understanding disease biology. Efforts to illuminate the
    basic mechanisms of ANCA-associated vasculitis are
    challenged by collecting sufficient numbers of biological
    samples from well characterised patients, and getting
    those samples into laboratories most capable of addressing
    translational questions specific to ANCA-associated
    vasculitis.

- PEXIVAS (2020)
    
    **Diseño**
    **Pacientes**: Vasculitis ANCA activa y grave inmunosupresión con CYC o RTX. Todos recibieron  hasta 3 gramos de metilprednisolona y luego la dosis inicial de corticoides de 50, 60 o 75 mg según peso, a partir de la primera semana empezaron a bajar. En ambos grupos, en la semana 22 se continuo con 5 mg hasta completa 30 semanas.
    
    Criterios de inclusión: > 15 años, PAM o GPA, VFG bajo 50, brote o rebrote (hematuria y empeoramiento de 25% de crea basal) y hemorragia alveolar.
    
    Diseño factorial 2 × 2, lo cual permite evaluaciones independientes del tratamiento inicial con recambios plasmáticos frente a aquellos pacientes que no reciben recambios y también permite la comparación de los 2 regímenes de glucocorticoides orale. 
    
    Para la comparación de recambios plasmáticos con recambios no plasmáticos, la hipótesis es que los recambios plasmáticos serían superiores.
    
    En los  2 brazos de esteroides, la hipótesis es que la dosis baja de esteroides no es inferior a la dosis estándar. 
    
    La aleatorización se realizó usando un algoritmo de minimización con estratificación según edad, función renal, subtipo de ANCA, gravedad de la hemorragia pulmonar y terapia de inducción recibida.
    
    Objetivo con recambio plasmático: fue un compuesto de muerte o falla renal terminal en seguimiento de 2,9 años.

    Falta de biopsia renal en la inclusión de pacientes. 
    
    La ausencia de información sobre la histología renal impide saber el grado de cronicidad del tejido renal de los pacientes incluidos. Probablemente, el efecto de los recambios plasmáticos sea beneficioso en los que no tienen cronicidad. 
    La otra limitación más relevante se relaciona con la definición del otro criterio de inclusión, la hemorragia alveolar. En el subgrupo de pacientes con hemorragia alveolar, 61 pacientes tienen una forma grave, definida como saturación de oxígeno < 85% al aire ambiente o necesidad de ventilación mecánica, mientras que 130 tienen una forma no grave. El análisis posterior de estos subgrupos de pacientes revela un posible efecto beneficioso de los recambios plasmáticos, aunque sin significación estadística.

Las guías Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) en el manejo de las glomerulonefritis (2020; no publicadas) recomendarán en el manejo de la VAA valorar el tratamiento con recambios plasmáticos en 2 situaciones: la afectación de un órgano vital y afectación renal aguda y grave (creatinina > 500 μmol/l o 5,6 mg/dl).
