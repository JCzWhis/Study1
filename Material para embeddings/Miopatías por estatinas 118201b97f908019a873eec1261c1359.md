### **Mialgia y leve hiperCKemia**

### Miopatía por estatinas autolimitada

Una forma más severa, en adición a la mialgia, puede tener debilidad simétrica.

### Rabdomiolisis

Rara, potencialmente fatal.

Dolor con o sin debilidad. 

Daño agudo muscular causa elevación rápida de CK (sobre 40 veces) y mioglobina sérica libre.

***Genética**
Genome-wide association studies have identified a strong association between the risk of developing toxic forms of statin myopathy and a non-synonymous single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) at rs4149056 in the SLCO1B1 gene on chromosome 12 [47]. This gene encodes the organic anion transporting polypeptide, OATP1B1, which acts as a transport molecule on the basolateral surface of hepatocytes. This SNP in question results in a decrease in function of the transporter, hence, increasing serum statin levels and potentiating myotoxicity. Individuals with the variant C allele have an odds ratio for myopathy of 4.5 per copy of the C allele (CI 95%, 2.6–7.7) and 16.9 (CI 95%, 4.7–61.1) if homozygous for the CC alleles as compared with the wild type TT genotype [48]. Although the SLCO1B1 SNP is an integral factor driving the risk of toxic statin myopathy, it does not explain much of the variability between patients. Perhaps some of its heritability may be associated with uncommon genetic variants with a large effect size. Alternatively, this may be explained by multiple more common variants with a small effect size that may be difficult to demonstrate in studies given the rarity of this entity [49].*

**Autoimmunidad**

SANAM es autoinmune. Asociado a  HLA DRB1�11:01 allele in adults. 

Hipótesis es que en individuos genéticamente predispuestos el bloqueo de HMGCR resulta en la sorbrexpresión de una molécula de HMGCR modificada y altamente inmunogénica. Esta incita la formación de autoanticuerpos.

Otra hipótesis es que las estatinas al unirse a la HMGCR inducen un cambio conformacional en la proteína que provoca que las células presentadoras de antígenos ofrezcan este péptido al sistema inmune como un antígeno extraño. 

In vitro, los antiHMGCR disminuyen la fusión y diferenciación de mioblastos, inducen atrofia de miofibrillas y aumentan la citoquinas inflamatorias como IL-16 y TNFalfa.

### **Patogénesis de la IMNM seropositiva**

Los ***anticuerpos anti-SRP y anti-HMGCR son patogénicos***

***Mecanismos propuestos de la patogénesis de la IMNM seropositiva***

![image.png](Miopati%CC%81as%20por%20estatinas%20118201b97f908019a873eec1261c1359/image%205.png)

Los autoanticuerpos anti-partícula de reconocimiento de señal (anti-SRP) o anti-3-hidroxi-3-metilglutaril-coA reductasa (anti-HMGCR) se unen a los autoantígenos ectópicamente expresados en la superficie celular y/o posiblemente penetran en las miofibras. La unión de autoanticuerpos a los antígenos activa la vía clásica del complemento. El ***reclutamiento de macrófagos*** conduce a la miocitofagia y a la liberación de citocinas proinflamatorias como IL-1, IL-6 y TNF. La regeneración muscular normal ocurre mediante la activación de células satélite residentes (es decir, mioblastos) que se dividen y participan en un proceso de fusión (formando miotubos) para generar miofibras maduras. La regeneración muscular se ve alterada por la interferencia de los autoanticuerpos en la diferenciación de los mioblastos y los bajos niveles de IL-4 e IL-13. La unión de los autoanticuerpos a los autoantígenos también desencadena mecanismos no inmunitarios, lo que lleva a la atrofia mediada por la ligasa de proteínas TRIM63 y la proteína F-box de atrofia muscular, y finalmente al daño muscular (no mostrado).

Primero, en la **IMNM seropositiva, el título de anticuerpos se correlaciona con la actividad de la enfermedad**. De hecho, tanto los títulos de anticuerpos anti-SRP como anti-HMGCR se correlacionan con la fuerza muscular y los niveles de creatina quinasa en suero, y los niveles de creatina quinasa en suero son un excelente biomarcador de la necrosis de las fibras musculares en pacientes con IMNM. En segundo lugar, la presencia de depósitos de complemento sarcolemal, así como signos de activación de la vía clásica del complemento, sugiere que la necrosis de las fibras musculares es dependiente de anticuerpos y complemento, mientras que se observan pocas o ninguna célula T citotóxica y/o células NK en biopsias musculares de pacientes con IMNM. De hecho, la cascada clásica del complemento se activa en pacientes con IMNM seropositiva, como lo atestigua la presencia de IgG1 y C1q y la formación del MAC C5b–9. En tercer lugar, los experimentos in vitro han demostrado que los autoanticuerpos anti-HMGCR y anti-SRP son patogénicos. Finalmente, los experimentos en ratones han mostrado que la transferencia pasiva de suero de pacientes con IMNM positiva para anti-HMGCR o anti-SRP induce una miopatía dependiente del complemento.

**Dianas de los autoanticuerpos**

Como ya se mencionó, los anticuerpos a***nti-SRP y anti-HMGCR tienen como diana proteínas ubicuas que están presentes en la superficie del retículo endoplásmico en el citoplasma de todas las células,*** lo que plantea dudas sobre el papel patogénico de los autoanticuerpos en una enfermedad autoinmune específica del músculo. Estos anticuerpos solo podrían volverse patogénicos si pudieran penetrar la membrana celular y alcanzar su diana, pero la evidencia creciente sugiere que ***SRP o HMGCR se encuentran ectópicamente presentes en la superficie de las fibras musculares***, donde pueden ser directamente atacados por sus anticuerpos. Primero, el ARN del autoantígeno SRP o HMGCR se expresa a altos niveles en células musculares humanas cultivadas, y la inmunohistoquímica ha demostrado que la superficie de los miotubos jóvenes en su fase regenerativa in vitro puede ser detectada por los anticuerpos anti-SRP o anti-HMGCR. Las mismas observaciones se han hecho en biopsias musculares de pacientes con IMNM y en músculos regenerantes de ratones, en los que la expresión del ARN del autoantígeno se correlaciona con la expresión de marcadores de regeneración muscular e inversamente con la expresión de genes que codifican proteínas musculares maduras. La inmunohistoquímica con autoanticuerpos anti-SRP o anti-HMGCR de pacientes también tiñe la superficie de las fibras musculares.

Estos hallazgos sugieren que algunas condiciones pueden permitir que los anticuerpos anti-SRP y HMGCR alcancen la superficie de las fibras musculares regenerativas, donde pueden ser atacados por anticuerpos anti-SRP o anti-HMGCR. Con esto en mente, debe destacarse el papel de las estatinas en el desencadenamiento de la IMNM positiva para anti-HMGCR, dado que, in vitro, los niveles de HMGCR son regulados al alza por las estatinas. Esta observación sugiere que la primera instancia de daño muscular y regeneración (por ejemplo, la miotoxicidad de las estatinas) puede inducir una respuesta inmune contra los antígenos musculares en pacientes con susceptibilidad genética. En consecuencia, algunos alelos del MHC de clase II DRB1 confieren una predisposición inmunogenética a la IMNM positiva para anti-HMGCR. Este hecho sugiere que los epítopos de SRP o HMGCR son presentados preferentemente por moléculas HLA-DR al sistema inmunológico, favoreciendo la ruptura de la tolerancia hacia estos autoantígenos y conduciendo a la producción de autoanticuerpos.

**Efectos de los autoanticuerpos in vitro**

Un estudio in vitro demostró que los anticuerpos anti-SRP y anti-HMGCR inducen atrofia de las fibras musculares con un aumento en la transcripción de genes que codifican factores atróficos como la proteína F-box de atrofia muscular (MAFBX; también conocida como atrogin 1) y la ligasa de proteínas E3 TRIM63 (también conocida como MURF1). Además, la co-cultura de fibras musculares con autoanticuerpos anti-SRP o anti-HMGCR se asoció con altos niveles de citocinas inflamatorias (como TNF e IL-6) y especies reactivas de oxígeno, lo que muestra que, incluso en ausencia de complemento, la unión de estos autoanticuerpos a sus dianas induce estrés celular en las miofibras. Además, los niveles de las citocinas antiinflamatorias IL-4 e IL-13 producidas por los mioblastos se redujeron en presencia de los autoanticuerpos anti-SRP y anti-HMGCR. La reducción de las citocinas antiinflamatorias disminuyó la formación de miotubos (IL-4 e IL-13 son citocinas clave en el mecanismo de fusión de los mioblastos) y este fenotipo se restauró mediante la adición de estas citocinas. Estas observaciones demuestran que, al menos in vitro, los autoanticuerpos anti-SRP y anti-HMGCR inducen atrofia muscular y alteran la regeneración muscular, lo cual es consistente con el mal pronóstico de los pacientes con IMNM seropositiva. En consonancia con este hecho, también se ha observado que la inhibición de la HMGCR por las estatinas disminuye la fusión de mioblastos in vitro y afecta la regeneración muscular esquelética in vivo.

**Efectos de los autoanticuerpos en ratones**

La naturaleza autoinmune de la IMNM seropositiva fue fuertemente sugerida por los resultados de un estudio de experimentos de transferencia adoptiva en un modelo murino. Esta estrategia se facilitó por la capacidad de los autoanticuerpos humanos anti-SRP y anti-HMGCR para reaccionar con antígenos ortólogos murinos. La IgG purificada del plasma de pacientes con IMNM seropositiva provocó una disminución en la fuerza muscular en ratones receptores, con cierto nivel de necrosis y regeneración de las fibras musculares. Este efecto fue claramente visible después de administrar IgG durante solo 7 días a ratones inmunosuprimidos transitoriamente (para limitar la xenorreactividad a la IgG humana) y el efecto fue más persistente cuando las inyecciones de IgG se prolongaron hasta el día 21 en ratones inmunodeficientes Rag2−/−. Similar a la transferencia pasiva de autoanticuerpos, la inmunización de ratones con SRP54 o HMGCR recombinantes también disminuyó la fuerza muscular, lo que confirma que la ruptura de la tolerancia hacia cada uno de los dos antígenos diana de la IMNM provoca una enfermedad muscular adquirida.

Statins and the nocebo effect
Indeed, the evidence shows that some patients on a statin with muscle symptoms and a normal CK rightfully attribute their symptoms to the statin, as corroborated by muscle biopsy revealing signs of toxicity. However, it has recently been demonstrated that many patients cannot distinguish between placebo and active statin therapy with regard to muscle- related symptoms. The SAMSON [78] and StatinWISE [79] trials demonstrate the important phenomenon of the ‘nocebo’ effect.
The SAMSON trial
The SAMSON study was a double-blinded, three-group, n- of-1 twelve-month study of 60 patients in the UK who reported statin-associated adverse events within two weeks of statin initiation. The study excluded those with a CK>5x ULN or an increased CK with myalgia. It was undertaken to determine whether muscle symptoms could be induced by a statin as well as by placebo. Patients were assigned four

bottles containing a statin, placebo tablets or no tablets to be used monthly in a random sequence while documenting their muscle symptom score from 0 to 100 each month. Interestingly, the difference in symptom severity reported for placebo compared with statin therapy was not clinically significant (15.4 vs 16.3, respectively, P<0.388). Ninety per cent of the symptoms induced by the statin were also experienced when patients were on placebo. However, the side effect severity was significant for both statin and placebo compared with no-tablet months (P for both <0.001). At the end of the study, half the participants successfully re- started statins.
The StatinWISE trial
Similarly, the StatinWISE study, a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled n-of-1 trial, revealed a primary outcome of atorvastatin 20mg having no overall effect on muscle symptoms compared with placebo in persons who previously reported SAMS. The year-long study was designed to include two-month treatment periods (three of placebo and three of active treatment) in a randomly allocated order. Patients reported muscle symptoms via a validated visual analogue scale for the last seven days of each treatment period. During statin periods, 9% of participants withdrew because of intolerable symptoms compared with 7% during placebo periods. The results showed no difference in mean muscle symptoms between statin and placebo periods. Of the patients who completed the study and attended an end-of-trial discussion, 66% stated that they had already resumed statin therapy or intended to. Of note, 15% of patients had a mean muscle symptom score at least 1 unit higher during a statin period of treatment and were thus informed that the statin may be contributing to their muscle symptoms; just over half of those patients said they planned to resume statin therapy anyway.
Enrolling patients who previously reported SAMS is a major strength of these studies which may empower physicians and patients to pursue statin re-challenges.

Drug–Drug interaction with statins
One of the more common reasons for statin myotoxicity is drug-drug interactions. This is not seen in SANAM but is observed only in the toxic forms of statin myopathy. There is a generally appreciated list of commonly offending drug classes, and such interactions vary among individual statins (Table 4). Thus, removing the offending drug interaction may be paramount to managing SAMS.

Statins and exercise
SAMS appear to be exacerbated by exercise. Several observational studies have shown decreased exercise tolerance in persons on statins and that SAMS were more profound in physically active than sedentary individuals [36, 80]. After a training session, it was noted that CK levels increased more in statin-treated athletes than in athletes not on therapy. Additionally, this association was not dose-dependent but rather related to increasing age [81]. Although there is clear evidence that exercise may worsen SAMS, a review of the literature reports conflicting results on the actual ability of statins to impair performance during exercise [82].

Normal CK does not always rule out toxic statin myopathy
CK levels are frequently normal in symptomatic patients taking statins, while they may also be increased in asymptomatic patients. A study was done with the aim of documenting normal CK levels in patients with biopsy-proven statin myopathy [3]. Four patients were studied under blinded conditions to receive either a statin or placebo. They were all able to identify blinded therapy consistently. Notably, all patients had a normal CK while on the statin. Their muscle biopsies showed increased lipid stores, cytochrome oxidase-negative myofibers and ragged red fibres suggestive of mitochondrial respiratory chain dysfunction in keeping with a myopathy. Three to five months following statin discontinuation, these findings had resolved (Fig. 2). For these reasons, CK is a non-sensitive biomarker for statin myopathy but is still used in evaluating SAMS due to the absence of other specific laboratory tests.

Concomitant neurologic diseases and statin toxicity
It is thought that patients with underlying neuromuscular disorders may be at a higher risk of developing SAMS, although much of this evidence stems from case reports; thus, a causal relationship cannot be established [47, 83]. This association was recognized in a report in which carriers for two of the commonest inherited myopathies were disproportionately affected by SAMS, i.e. PYGM and CPT2 carriers had a 20- and 13-fold risk, respectively [84]. In a study of 54 patients with myasthenia gravis on a statin, 6 (11%) developed worsening disease, half of which resolved with statin withdrawal and the rest required additional treatment to achieve remission [85]. Another study showed that patients with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis on a statin had an increase in the rate of functional decline and muscle cramping compared with controls [86].
A few case reports have also documented the unmasking of other neuromuscular diseases thought to be in a subclinical state by statins. These include polymyositis, dermatomyositis, acid maltase deficiency, myotonic dystrophy, McArdle disease, mitochondrial myopathies, Kennedy disease and MELAS (mitochondrial encephalopathy, lactic acidosis and stroke-like episodes) [87–93]. Despite these potential associations, there is no absolute contraindication for using statins in patients with underlying neuromuscular disorders, particularly if the benefit of their use outweighs the risk. The only absolute contraindication to statin use is in SANAM.

The SPORT trial
By 2019, 77% of US adults were taking dietary supplements, of which 17% were taking them for ‘heart health’. Despite their popularity, the data to suggest these are effective is lacking. The SPORT trial compared the lipid-lowering effects of low-dose rosuvastatin to placebo and six commonly used supplements: fish oil, garlic, cinnamon, turmeric, plant sterols and red yeast rice [97]. Compared with placebo, the reduction in LDL-C from rosuvastatin 5mg was −35.2% (95% CI: −41.3% to −29.1%; P <0.001). In addition, there was no statistically significant decrease in the LDL-C from any of the supplements compared with placebo.
Naturally occurring statins
As previously mentioned, not all patients with anti-HMGCR antibodies and necrotizing myopathy have taken statins. It is theorized that some of these patients perhaps have been exposed to foods or supplements containing statin-like compounds like red yeast rice, oyster mushrooms and Pu Erh Tea [2]. One case report documented anti-HMGCR myopathy in a young male five years after consistently taking Bacopa supplements, which is known to have lipid-lowering effects through inhibition of HMGCR activity [98].

### Casos clínicos

*Alex, a 71-year-old man with a history of ASCVD, hypertension,
hyperlipidemia and type 2 diabetes mellitus was evaluated
by his primary care physician (PCP) for pre-procedural
labs for a scheduled liver biopsy, which his cardiologist recommended.
A biopsy was recommended to further evaluate
persistently elevated liver-associated enzymes (AST, ALT) for
the past four months. He had been taking atorvastatin 80 mg
daily for 31 months. Alex reported extreme fatigue and muscle
soreness for the past two months, difficulty climbing
stairs, getting off low-seated chairs and washing his hair in
the shower. He denied frank dysphagia but had noticed occasionally
having to ‘double swallow’ to swallow dry bread. He
also denied having joint pain or Raynaud’s phenomenon and
has no family history of autoimmune diseases. Despite stopping
the atorvastatin, his creatine kinase (CK) continued
to rise and he became weaker in the ensuing month. He
was noted to have weakness of the deltoids and hip flexors
on examination. His primary care physician recognized that
a CK had never been ascertained, so she repeated the
comprehensive metabolic panel and added a CK. AST was
148 U/l (normal range 5–34 U/l), ALT was 172 U/l (normal
range < 55 U/l) and the CK was markedly elevated at
5250 IU/l (normal range < 250 IU/l).*

*His cousin, Todd, is 63 and also had difficulty with statinrelated
side effects. Todd had stopped atorvastatin 80 mg
daily after six weeks of use because his muscles were painful.
He reported fatigue but denied true weakness, and had calf
and other diffuse muscle pain on exam. His AST was 24 U/l
(normal range 5–34 U/l), and ALT was 40 (normal range
<55 U/l). His CK was 450 U/l; pre-statin CK was unknown.
His CK normalized after the statin was discontinued but rose
upon re-challenging with atorvastatin at any dose and with
rosuvastatin at higher doses. He was able to tolerate lowdose
rosuvastatin with the addition of a PCSK9 inhibitor for
excellent lipid control.
