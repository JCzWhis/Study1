Cardiomyocyte electrophysiology during hypokalemia. (1) Hypokalemia inhibits outward potassium currents (transient outward
K+ current [Ito], rapidly activating delayed rectifier K+ current [IKr], slowly activating delayed rectifier K+ current [IKs], inward rectifier K+ current
[IK1]) and the Na/K ATPase pump current (INaK) with consequent loss of repolarization reserve and increased intracellular Na. (2) High
intracellular [Na+] inhibits Ca2+ removal via the Na/Ca exchanger (NCX), and Ca2+ accumulates in the cytosol. (3) High [Ca2+]cytosol activates
Ca/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII), which increases ICa and late INa inward currents. (4) Together with the hypokalemia
(HypoK)-induced block of K+ currents, these changes in ionic currents cause action potential (AP) prolongation and promote early afterdepolarization
(EAD)– and delayed afterdepolarization (DAD)–triggered arrhythmias. (5) During hypokalemia, the increased [Ca2+]cytosol
activates apamin-sensitive IKAS currents, which shorten the AP. When IKAS is inhibited by apamin, AP is not restored, and ventricular
arrhythmogenesis ensues.

El movimiento del potasio desde la célula cardiaca está impulsado por dos gradientes principales:

1. **Gradiente de concentración**:
    - **Interior de la célula**: La concentración de potasio es alta.
    - **Exterior de la célula**: La concentración de potasio es baja.
    - Este gradiente de concentración favorece la salida de potasio de la célula.
2. **Gradiente eléctrico**:
    - **Potencial de membrana en reposo**: Es negativo, típicamente alrededor de -90 mV en las células cardiacas.
    - **Carga del potasio**: Positiva.
    - El potencial negativo dentro de la célula atrae a los iones de potasio hacia adentro, oponiéndose al gradiente de concentración.

La combinación de estos gradientes se conoce como **gradiente electroquímico** y determina la dirección y magnitud del flujo de potasio a través de la membrana celular.

En resumen, el movimiento del potasio desde la célula cardiaca está impulsado principalmente por el gradiente de concentración que favorece su salida y el gradiente eléctrico que tiende a retenerlo dentro de la célula.

**Hiperkalemia**

Sistémica o intersticial (local, en el corazón)

Sistémica

→ Potencial de membrana en reposo y equilibrio de potasio se hacen menos negativos

→ Hace más corto el potencial de acción y altera la velocidad de conducción

por: Aumenta conductancia de potasio, creando una reserva de repolarización en exceso

→ Baja el periodo refractario, pero si la hiperkalemia es severa, la conducción alta de potasio puede producir **refractariedad posrepolarización**

En hiperkalemia, donde la concentración de potasio extracelular está elevada, ocurren varios cambios en la dinámica del potasio en las células cardiacas:

1. **Reducción del gradiente de concentración**:
    - **Interior de la célula**: La concentración de potasio sigue siendo alta.
    - **Exterior de la célula**: La concentración de potasio está aumentada.
    - Esto reduce el gradiente de concentración que normalmente favorece la salida de potasio de la célula, disminuyendo la fuerza impulsora para que el potasio salga.
2. **Cambio en el potencial de membrana**:
    - **Despolarización**: El aumento de potasio extracelular reduce la negatividad del potencial de membrana en reposo, llevándolo a un valor menos negativo (despolarización).
    - Esta despolarización puede inactivar algunos canales de potasio sensibles al voltaje, afectando la conductancia de potasio.
3. **Impacto en el gradiente eléctrico**:
    - La despolarización de la membrana reduce el atractivo eléctrico que normalmente retiene al potasio dentro de la célula, lo que puede llevar a una salida más fácil del potasio si los canales están abiertos.
4. **Conductancia de potasio**:
    - **Aumento inicial**: La despolarización puede abrir ciertos canales de potasio, incrementando inicialmente la conductancia.
    - **Inactivación de canales**: La prolongada despolarización puede llevar a la inactivación de algunos canales de potasio, como los canales de corriente transitoria de salida (Ito), disminuyendo la conductancia neta con el tiempo.

**Consecuencias clínicas de la hiperkalemia**:

- **Ritmo cardiaco**: La despolarización persistente puede causar arritmias, ya que la excitabilidad del miocardio está alterada.
- **Potencial de acción**: Puede acortarse, y la fase de repolarización puede verse afectada, alterando la propagación del impulso eléctrico en el corazón.

How does calcium "stabilize the cardiac membrane" in hyperkalemia?

I learned early in my intern year to use calcium in the setting of severe hyperkalemia.

I never really learned how it works. The answer requires some history. And uncovers a forgotten alternative treatment.

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/En6qGjvWEAEGkbi.png)

2/
First, some history.

While Sidney Ringer was developing his eponymous fluid, he observed that increasing potassium content led to progressively weaker ventricular contractions.

He reported these findings in 1883.

 [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.go/)

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/En6qUElXYAQCxCc.jpg)

3/
How does hyperkalemia affect the heart? To understand the answer, recall that the generation of an action potential is dependent on the:

(1) resting membrane potential (−90mV for myocytes)
(2) threshold potential (-70mV)
(3) activation state of membrane sodium channels

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/En6qcnsXcAAyta2.jpg)

4/
And also recall that the resting membrane potential results from the outward leak of potassium.

This leak of positively charged ions leaves the inner membrane relatively negative.

 [pathophys.org](http://www.pathophys.org/physi)

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/En6qj0rXYAA6VsZ.jpg)

5/

![](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@14.0.2/assets/72x72/1f511.png)

As the serum potassium increases, the gradient for outward flow diminishes. This leaves the resting potential LESS negative... 

...and closer to the threshold potential.

Result:

![](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@14.0.2/assets/72x72/1f4a5.png)

Increased myocyte excitability

![](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@14.0.2/assets/72x72/1f4a5.png)

You might even say it is "unstable".

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/En6qpVJXUAA5eIQ.jpg)

6/
How does calcium "stabilize" this unstable (excitable) membrane? There are at least two options:

(1) Decrease the resting potential (i.e., make it MORE negative)
(2) Increase the threshold potential (i.e., move it further away from the resting potential)

Which is it?

7/
In 1955 Silvio Weidmann showed that MORE depolarization was required in calcium-rich solutions.

![](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@14.0.2/assets/72x72/1f511.png)

The resting potential was unchanged, but the threshold potential had INCREASED. 

He concluded that "this accounts for the 'stabilizing' effect of Ca."

[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.go/)

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/En6qz3hXEAYtPkB.jpg)

8/
Interim Summary
➢ Hyperkalemia increases the resting potential, bringing it closer to the threshold potential
➢ This makes myocytes excitable/unstable
➢ Calcium increases the threshold potential, moving it further from the resting potential, stabilizing the myocyte membrane

9/
Again, hyperkalemia increases the resting potential leading to INCREASED membrane excitability.

But...

...a persistent increase in resting potential inactivates sodium channels required for Phase 0. This leads to DECREASED membrane excitability.

 [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.go/)

10/
The decrease in membrane excitability is shown by a decreased rate of rise (Vmax) of phase 0 of the action potential.

Why is Vmax slower?

![](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@14.0.2/assets/72x72/1f511.png)

The resting potential during depolarization determines the number of active sodium channels.

[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.go/)

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/En6reTEXMAMuwYu.jpg)

11/
At -90 mV (the normal resting potential), the maximum number of channels are open. 

As the resting potential increases (as with hyperkalemia), fewer channels are open leading to slower depolarization (i.e., decreased Vmax).

 [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.go/)

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/En6rZbYXUAAufi1.jpg)

12/
To protect against this second mechanism, any intervention must mitigate the inactivation of sodium channels.

Calcium does this!

It increases the activity of Nav 1.5 sodium channels required for Phase 0 depolarization.

 [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.go/)

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/En6s0D2XcAEp9ER.jpg)

13/
Another treatment has been shown to mitigate the effects of hyperkalemia.

Since at least 1918, hypertonic saline has been shown effective at combatting the cardiotoxic effects of hyperkalemia.

I had never heard of this as a treatment.

 [jpet.aspetjournals.org](https://jpet.aspetjournals.org/)

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/En6s4lJXYAA2nJf.jpg)

14/
Just as with calcium, hypertonic saline can increase Vmax via activation of Nav 1.5 sodium channels.

Simplistically, the increased extracellular sodium concentration increases the velocity of flow across the cell membrane when the channels open.

 [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.go/)

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/En6s-12XMAA3vBH.jpg)

15/
Hypertonic saline can have similar effects to those seen with calcium.

For example, you can see improvement in the ECG abnormalities caused by hyperkalemia. So cool!

 [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.go/)

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/En6tGtOXUAE938w.jpg)
