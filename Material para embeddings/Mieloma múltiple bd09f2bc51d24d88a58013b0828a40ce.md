- Fisiopatología
    
    Las celulas de mieloma derivan desde el centro postgerminal de las celulas B. 
    
    En individuos sanos, luego de la exposición al antígeno, las células B naive normalmente prolifern y hacen hipermutación somática de IgH and IgL VDJ sequences. Esto produce células plamática de vida larga (un subgrupo de celulas plasmáticas que provee producción de anticuerpo sostenida y por largo tiempo) que van a residir a MO.
    
    Una células plasmática clonal anormal simula este proceso fisiológico pero con Ig excesiva.
    
    En casi todos los pacientes inicia como MGUS, una discracia de 3-5% en >65 años, 10% >80 años. Progresa 1-2% al año
    
    Smoldering multiple myeloma (SMM) is a more advanced plasma cell disorder, Approximately 10% of patients with SMM progress to multiple myeloma each year. Se deben monitorizar cada 3 a 6 meses con hemoglobin, serum creatinine, serum calcium, and serum free light chain levels; serum and urine protein electrophoresis with immunofixation; and whole-body skeletal imaging, until disease complications are identified. Treatment with lenalidomide should be considered for patients with SMM who are at higher risk of progression to symptomatic multiple myeloma, to delay or avoid development of multiple myeloma complications.
    
    In 2013, randomized 119 participants tolenalidomide + dexamethasone vs observation alone. At a median follow-up of 40 months, 13 of 57 patients in the treatment group (23%) developed multiple myeloma, vs 47 of 62 patients in the observation group (76%). During the specified study period, 4 of 57 patients in the treatment group (7%) died, vs 13 of 62 (21%) in the observation group. At a median follow-up of 40 months, the hazard ratio (HR) for developing multiple myeloma with treatment was 0.18 (95% CI, 0.09-0.32; P < .001). Compared with observation, 3-year survival was better in the group receiving lenalidomide + dexamethasone (HR for death, 0.31 [95% CI, 0.10- 0.91]; P = .03).
    A second clinical trial17 randomized 182 patients with highrisk
    SMM to either placebo or lenalidomide, continued until progression
    or intolerance of therapy occurred. At 3 years of followup,
    8 patients in the 90-patient treatment group had progressed
    to multiple myeloma compared with 31 patients in the 92-patient
    observation group. Overall mortality was low in both groups over
    the 36-month study period; therefore, a statistically significant
    difference in survival was not demonstrated. Two patients died in
    the treatment group and 4 patients in the observation group.

    In patients newly diagnosed with multiple myeloma, approximately 3.3% present with extramedullary disease (defined as the presence of 1 or more extraosseous plasmacytomas on crosssectional imaging), central nervous system involvement, or plasma cell leukemia. Extramedullary disease is associated with a more aggressive disease course, both at time of diagnosis and among patients with relapsed multiple myeloma. Central nervous systeminvolvement generally presents as either leptomeningeal disease or cranial nerve involvement. Treatment consists of administration of intrathecal chemotherapy with cytarabine,methotrexate, or both, and survival is typically short (approximately 7 months) Plasma cell leukemia is defined by presence of 20%or greater plasma cells in the peripheral blood and is associated with a 4-year survival of 28%,evenamongpatients receivingautologoushematopoietic stem cell transplantation.

    Autologous Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation
    for Multiple Myeloma
    For patients undergoing stem cell transplantation, colonystimulating
    factor, chemotherapy (typically cyclophosphamide), or
    both is used to stimulate and mobilize peripheral blood stem cells,
    which are collected using apheresis. After stem cell collection, patients
    receive intravenous high-dose melphalan (a potent antimyeloma
    agent), followed by reinfusion of stem cells. The Intergroupe
    Francophone du Myélome 2009 clinical trial randomized
    700patients with newly diagnosed multiplemyeloma treated with
    RVd induction and consolidation to either transplant or no transplant,
    followed by maintenance lenalidomide for 1 year. At 14-
    month follow-up, progression-free survivalwas significantly better
    in people who received transplant (50 months vs 36 months; adjusted
    HR for disease progression or death, 0.65 [95% CI, 0.53-
    0.80]). A separate clinical trial conducted in Europe, the EMN02/
    HO95 trial, randomized 1197 patients to either transplant or
    bortezomib + melphalan + prednisone consolidation after induction
    with bortezomib + cyclophosphamide + dexamethasone.81The
    median progression-free survivalwas significantly better with transplant
    (56.7 months vs 41.9 months; HR, 0.73 [95%CI, 0.62-0.85]).
    These randomized clinical trials support continued benefit for transplant
    after induction for eligible patients with multiplemyeloma. In
    patients with high-risk multiple myeloma, studies have suggested
    that atandemautologous stemcell transplant (performanceof a second
    transplant approximately 3 months after the first) may improve
    overall survival and progression-free survival. The role of tandem
    transplantation for multiplemyeloma is under study.92
    Mortality rates from transplant are less than or equal to 1%.88
    The most common adverse effects of transplant include mucositis,
    nausea/vomiting, anorexia, andmyelosuppression (associated with
    higher risk of infection).85 There is a small increase in secondary malignancies,
    particularly myelodysplastic syndrome and acute myeloid
    leukemia, in patients receiving long-term lenalidomide maintenance
    therapy after transplant.93
    Reduced-intensity allogeneic (donor and recipient are not the
    same person) hematopoietic stem cell transplantation using donor
    stemcells is not widely used for treatment of multiplemyeloma, and
    
    clinical trial data are conflicting regarding benefit.94 Allogeneic hematopoietic
    stem cell transplantation for multiple myeloma is primarilyusedforyounger
    patients (usuallyyounger than50years)who
    have high-risk disease and should be conducted within clinical trials
    whenever possible.
    After transplant, the standard of standard care is maintenance
    therapy (defined as long-term continuous low-dose administration
    of an active antimyeloma agent) with either lenalidomide or
    bortezomib to prolong remission. Maintenance therapy with
    lenalidomide improved survival in a single large trial andwas associated

    Minimal Residual Disease in Multiple Myeloma
    New technologies are available to evaluate patients for minimal residual
    disease (ie, very low levels of multiple myeloma in the bone
    marrow after therapy). Next-generation sequencing of the immunoglobulin
    gene within myeloma cells allows detection of malignant
    plasma cells present at 1 abnormal cell per million normal cells
    (10−6) and is now approved by the US Food and Drug Administration
    (FDA) for multiple myeloma.98 Minimal residual disease assessed
    by next-generation sequencing can be used to predict longterm
    outcomes, and its potential application in treatment is under
    study ([ClinicalTrials.gov](http://clinicaltrials.gov/) identifiers NCT03901963 and
    NCT04071457).
    Treatment of Relapsed Multiple Myeloma
    Many therapeutic options are available for patients with relapsed
    and refractory multiple myeloma (Table 3). If not previously received,
    treatment with daratumumab or isatuximab—both anti-
    CD38 monoclonal antibodies—combined with a steroid and either
    an immunomodulatory drug or a proteasome inhibitor, canbehighly
    effective, with overall response rates of 60% to 85%.67-70,99,100
    Carfilzomib, a second-generation proteasome inhibitor, may be
    combined with an immunomodulatory agent or alkylating agent
    (cyclophosphamide).59-62,84 Elotuzumab, an anti-SLAMF7 (surface
    antigen CD319) monoclonal antibody, is effectivewhenadministered
    in combination with dexamethasone and either lenalidomide
    or pomalidomide.71,73 Selinexor, an exportin 1 inhibitor, was
    approved in 2019 to treat patients with relapsed multiple myeloma.
    In 2020, belantamab mafodotin, a monoclonal antibody–
    drug conjugate that targets a protein on the surface of plasma cells,
    B-cell maturation antigen,was approved by theFDA.74,75,77 Bendamustine
    has both alkylating and antimetabolite activity and is effective
    for some patients.101 Multiagent cytotoxic chemotherapy
    (eg, cyclophosphamide + doxorubicin + cisplatin + etoposide
    [PACE], or other similar regimens) can be administered for shortterm
    disease control or as a bridge tomoredurable therapies insome
    patients with rapidly progressive disease.102
    Transplant should be considered at the time of disease relapse
    in patients with stored autologous stem cells who have achieved a
    durable remission from their first transplant; patients who postponed
    transplant at diagnosis should be strongly considered for this
    procedure at first relapse.103
    In April 2021, the first chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell
    therapy targeting the B-cell surface maturation antigen for multiple
    myeloma, idecaptagene vicleucel (Ide-cel/Abecma), was approvedby
    theFDAfor patients with relapsed multiplemyeloma.79,80
    In CAR T-cell therapy, T cells are collected from a patient’s bloodstream
    and then genetically engineered to target a surface protein
    onthemyeloma cellmembrane.TheengineeredCARTcells are then
    administered back to the patient after immunosuppressive chemotherapy
    (to prevent the immune system from destroying the new
    T cells). Bispecific T-cell engagers, engineered molecules that bind
    and activate T cells to stimulate their targeting of malignant cells,
    are being tested in advanced clinical trials with success in relapsed
    multiplemyeloma.104 In addition,newderivatives ofimmunomodulatory
    drugs, known as cereblon E3 ligase modulators (CELMoDs),
    have been tested in early phase studies and shown early evidence
    of efficacyandsafetyinrelapsedandrefractorymultiplemyeloma.105

    Plasmocitomas solitarios:
    a) Óseo: el volumen de radiación debe incluir todo el hueso más 2 a 3 cm de margen, para dar una dosis de 40 a 50 Gy en 4,5 a 5 semanas.
    b) Extramedular: debe incluir la zona de drenaje linfático primaria. Dosis: 45 a 60 Gy en 4,5 a 6 semanas.

    c) Para lesiones óseas sintomáticas las dosis pueden variar de 15 a 20 Gy en 5 sesiones, 30 Gy en 10 sesiones o una fracción de 8 Gy, dependiendo de las condiciones generales del paciente. Incluir todo el hueso comprometido sólo en el caso de que no vaya a afectar una eventual recolección de células madre o terapias posteriores; en lesiones de columna vertebral agregar 1 a 2 vértebras por sobre y bajo la lesión.
    d) Lesiones líticas grandes (> de 3 cm) en huesos que soportan peso requieren de fijación quirúrgica. La radioterapia puede ser administrada pre o post fijación; se prefiere esta última.

- Seguimiento
    
    6.- SEGUIMIENTO
    Pacientes en tratamiento:
    o Se recomienda control mensual con exámenes de laboratorio general y EFP en suero si tienen peak monoclonal al diagnóstico.
    o Una vez que la EFP se normaliza, se recomienda evaluación con inmunofijación y/o cadenas livianas libres para comprobar la magnitud de la respuesta.
    o Mielograma al momento de la mejor respuesta con (de preferencia) o sin citometría de flujo.
    o Pacientes con MM de cadenas livianas se recomienda control con proteinuria de 24 h mensual y EFP en orina cada 2 meses. Cuando la EFP en orina y proteinuria de 24 h son normales se recomienda medir inmunofijación en orina y/o cadenas livianas libres para comprobar magnitud de la respuesta.
    o Mielograma al momento de la mejor respuesta con (de preferencia) o sin citometría de flujo.
    o Pacientes con MM no secretor se recomienda seguimiento con determinación de cadenas livianas.
    o Pacientes con poco componente monoclonal en sangre, pero con predominio de enfermedad ósea se recomienda seguimiento con determinación de cadenas livianas, que tienen mejor correlación con la masa tumoral.
    o Pacientes con MM de cadenas livianas con falla renal, en diálisis, se recomienda determinación de cadenas livianas.
