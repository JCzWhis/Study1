**Mecanismos de reemplazo renal**

1. **Difusión**:
    - Movimiento pasivo de solutos desde una área de mayor concentración a una de menor concentración a través de una membrana semipermeable.
    - **Aplicación en diálisis**: Es el principal mecanismo en la hemodiálisis convencional, donde las toxinas y productos de desecho en la sangre se difunden hacia el líquido de diálisis.
2. **Ultrafiltración**:
    - Eliminación de líquidos mediante la aplicación de una presión positiva o negativa a través de una membrana semipermeable.
    - **Aplicación en diálisis**: Utilizado para remover el exceso de líquido del cuerpo del paciente durante la diálisis, regulando el volumen de fluidos.
3. **Convección**:
    - Movimiento de solutos arrastrados por el flujo de agua (solvente) a través de una membrana semipermeable.
    - **Aplicación en diálisis**: Es un mecanismo importante en la hemofiltración y la hemodiafiltración, donde las grandes moléculas son removidas junto con el flujo de agua.
4. **Hemofiltración**:
    - **Definición**: Técnica de depuración extracorpórea donde se utiliza la convección para remover grandes volúmenes de agua y solutos a través de una membrana semipermeable.
    - **Aplicación**: Comúnmente usada en pacientes con insuficiencia renal aguda, proporciona una eliminación eficiente de solutos de mediano y gran tamaño.

**Hemodialysis and hemofiltration**

### **Basic principles [2][9][10]**

- **Hemodialysis**: utilizes **diffusion** and a small degree of ultrafiltration to remove solutes and water from the blood
    - More effective at removing small molecules (e.g., [urea](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/FK0gRS#IlowNGQ1MzVlMDhhZTIxYzExMjYyODQ4NmUwMzY2NjRhMSI=), [creatinine](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/kg0mv2#IlozMGY3MDg0MDA4OTQwNzY0NzYyZjc5MmY3ZThhZWJkZCI=), [ammonia](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/FK0gRS#IlpmNjE4NmE4NTg3ZGU3NWQxMjlkMjQ1MGY1NmQyMzdiNyI=)) than larger molecules
    - Blood is pumped through the dialysis unit on one side of a semipermeable membrane and dialysate in the opposite direction on the other side of the membrane.
    - Molecules diffuse across the semipermeable membrane down their concentration gradient.
- **Hemofiltration**: utilizes **convection** and a high degree of ultrafiltration to remove solutes and water from the blood
    - More effective at removing medium to large molecules (e.g., [cytokines](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/4p03pS#IloxYTliNTdhMjAxMGYyNzRmNGEwZjQ5ZTYwMTBkMzBjZiI=), [myoglobin](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/WS0PA2#IlpiNTUwZmY0MTYwZGI3ZTgyM2NkYWUzOWE1MDMzNzYwOSI=)) than [hemodialysis](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#IlpiY2NkNjI3OTIyYzNhYzM5NzgyY2I2NzVlZDRjMWJjZCI=)
        
        [10]
        
    - Blood is pumped through a machine and the difference in hydrostatic pressures drives water and solutes across a semipermeable membrane.
    - Lost plasma volume is replaced with a physiologic [crystalloid solution](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/fM0kLg#IlpjODBhMzI1ZTM5Y2NjNWYxYTU1YzM0ODYzNTc3MGI2NyI=).
        
        [2]
        
- **Hemodiafiltration**
    - Combination of [hemodialysis](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#IlpiY2NkNjI3OTIyYzNhYzM5NzgyY2I2NzVlZDRjMWJjZCI=) and [hemofiltration](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#Ilo0Mzc4N2QyOGIwZTAzMWIyMjUxNGE2ZGI0MTY4ZDhlYiI=)
    - Removes molecules of all sizes (most effective method for medium-sized molecule clearance)
- **Administration options**
    - Intermittent renal replacement therapy
        - Typically occurs over 3–5 hours; may last 6–18 hours (prolonged intermittent renal replacement therapy)
            
            [2]
            
            [7]
            
        - Most common option for outpatient administration in patients with [CKD](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/lg0vv2#IlplYjk3NTg4OGNkZTQ1MTU2NzJhYjg5YTdhMzA2OGFiMiI=); also used for [acute RRT](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#IlpkMTBkODcxNmYxNWUwYjZkZmI4Yjg5N2VhMWYwMmYwOSI=)
            
            [7]
            
    - Continuous renal replacement therapy
        
        [7]
        
        - Gradual fluid and solute clearance over 24 hours
        - Used almost exclusively for [acute RRT](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#IlpkMTBkODcxNmYxNWUwYjZkZmI4Yjg5N2VhMWYwMmYwOSI=)
        - Preferred if fluid shifts are contraindicated
            
            [3]
            

[Intermittent RRT](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#IlpjZDNjYWM4N2FkNDdmOTIwMjYyM2NhM2VkYWUzZTZhMyI=) can be performed in a clinic or at home by the patient after adequate training.

### **Access [1][3][11]**

Consider the anticipated duration of RRT and patient factors (e.g., preference, life expectancy, mobility) to determine the most appropriate type of access for RRT.

### **Venous catheter for renal replacement therapy**

- **Indications**
    
    [1]
    
    [11]
    
    - Urgent and/or short-term RRT
    - If an [AV fistula](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/2i0TIf#IlpkNzVlY2NmNGU2ODY1OGJmYjczODY3OWY5YjI0NDVhZSI=) or [AV graft](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#Ilo2ZjUzODE0N2ViOTBiNjM0NTZmNDBlZjg5NzQ0MDg0NiI=) cannot be used
    - Limited life expectancy
- **Types of catheter**
    
    [1]
    
    [3]
    
    - Nontunneled hemodialysis catheter
        - Typically used as initial access in urgent [acute RRT](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#IlpkMTBkODcxNmYxNWUwYjZkZmI4Yjg5N2VhMWYwMmYwOSI=)
        - High infection risk; use limited to a maximum of 2 weeks
            
            [11]
            
    - [Tunneled hemodialysis catheter](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/BG0zY3#IlowNGViODk4OWYwN2Q4NzhkMDI5YzlkY2RjYjZhM2JkNyI=)
        - Lower infection risk and more stable than nontunneled catheters
        - Suitable for long-term use
- **Placement**: [central venous access](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/Ut0b13#IlpjOTdkNmVhMGExOGZiMzVjMjdiYjE1MjNlNTQyODA1ZSI=), preferably in the right [internal jugular vein](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/b60HjS#IlplZGY1NTc2ZjRhMGM0ZDU2MzViNGZhNDJkMjU0NmY5ZiI=)
    
    [1]
    
    [3]
    
    [11]
    
- **Structure**
    
    [9]
    
    - Large bore, double-lumen
    - Blood is removed from the [vein](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/ZK0ZUS#Ilo2ZjhkNWYzNTM3ZjQ0YzdiZDdmMTkxNWQ3MjZiNjM2MCI=) through one lumen and returned through the other lumen.

### **Arteriovenous access [11]**

- **Indication**: for [maintenance dialysis](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#IlpjMTYwMTk4YTZhNjYxODFmYWRhMjZjZjBhMmNhNjk3ZSI=) in [ESRD](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/lg0vv2#Ilo1ZjJkMmIxNDhkNTc1NGRkNDY1MjljOTI5YTgzNjhlZSI=)
- **Procedure**
    - An anastomosis is created between an [artery](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/ZK0ZUS#IlpmNjdmOGJlNWQ4ZTJiNTc3Y2NjMzQ1NzVjMTk0MGRmOCI=) and a [vein](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/ZK0ZUS#Ilo2ZjhkNWYzNTM3ZjQ0YzdiZDdmMTkxNWQ3MjZiNjM2MCI=) to provide long-term large-bore [vascular access](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/BG0zY3#Ilo5ODlkZWNmNzVjMWVkNDQxNmRkMGI1NDNlYWQxNTFmOSI=)
    - Usually created in the nondominant arm (less impairment) between the [radial artery](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/OJ0I8S#IlphNGViZWQ2Y2FjOGVhNzFiZjgyZDkyZDA1OTBkMGQzMiI=) and the [cephalic vein](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/OJ0I8S#IlpjMDkzMDM4OGIwMTMzMjQ4MzY1MGVjNGI2YTRlZTBmNyI=) (i.e., radiocephalic fistula)
- **Types of AV access**
    - [Arteriovenous fistula](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/2i0TIf#IlpkNzVlY2NmNGU2ODY1OGJmYjczODY3OWY5YjI0NDVhZSI=): an [artery](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/ZK0ZUS#IlpmNjdmOGJlNWQ4ZTJiNTc3Y2NjMzQ1NzVjMTk0MGRmOCI=) is directly connected to a [vein](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/ZK0ZUS#Ilo2ZjhkNWYzNTM3ZjQ0YzdiZDdmMTkxNWQ3MjZiNjM2MCI=)
    - Arteriovenous graft: a synthetic or biological conduit is used to connect an [artery](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/ZK0ZUS#IlpmNjdmOGJlNWQ4ZTJiNTc3Y2NjMzQ1NzVjMTk0MGRmOCI=) to a [vein](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/ZK0ZUS#Ilo2ZjhkNWYzNTM3ZjQ0YzdiZDdmMTkxNWQ3MjZiNjM2MCI=)

[AV fistulas](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/2i0TIf#IlpkNzVlY2NmNGU2ODY1OGJmYjczODY3OWY5YjI0NDVhZSI=) need approximately 4–6 weeks to mature (i.e., enlargement and thickening of the [vein](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/ZK0ZUS#Ilo2ZjhkNWYzNTM3ZjQ0YzdiZDdmMTkxNWQ3MjZiNjM2MCI=) in response to arterial pressure); maturation is unsuccessful in up to 60% of patients. Arrange AV access before dialysis is likely to be required. [11]

### **Complications of hemodialysis [2][7][11]**

### [**Vascular access](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/BG0zY3#Ilo5ODlkZWNmNzVjMWVkNDQxNmRkMGI1NDNlYWQxNTFmOSI=) complications [11]**

- Loss of access due to thrombosis or stenosis
- Infections e.g., [skin and soft tissue infection](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/qh0CUf#IlozNWUxYmQ0OTYxZDc0ZjEyZWQyZTIzOTlmMjE5YWFhOSI=), [central line-associated bloodstream infection](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/g70FOh#IlozZTVjNGUyZDhjZmI0OGE5YWZhMGU1NzU3NjUyM2M0NiI=)
- Local [aneurysm](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/Wh0P1f#Ilo4YjZmMDJjMzkwZGM0OGMzNDExNmViYTY4NjYyZjkyYiI=)
- AV access steal syndrome: painful [ischemia](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/VP0GdT#IlpiYzA1ZTU0NGQ0NmU3OTRhNjBhYzljZTllYzY4ODNhZiI=) of the hand secondary to the [AV fistula](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/2i0TIf#IlpkNzVlY2NmNGU2ODY1OGJmYjczODY3OWY5YjI0NDVhZSI=) or graft shunting blood away from the [distal](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/xo0EVS#IlpjZTIzMmY5NzhlYTVhOGZiOWVkYmI5ODBhNDE1MDJiOCI=) limb
- Dialysis vascular access hemorrhage
    
    [12]
    
    [13]
    
    [14]
    
    - Apply firm pressure for 15–20 minutes; avoid occluding the vessel.
    - If the patient is hemodynamically unstable:
        - [Manage as hemorrhagic shock](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/kO0msT#IlplMjEyNDU3MjhjOGMzZmY5NGI3ODFhNmQ3MjA5ZWFiNCI=)
        - Place tourniquets above and below the site and attempt a figure of 8 or purse-string suture.
        - Determine time of last dialysis and consider [anticoagulant reversal](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/7q04-S#Ilo2YTRhOTdhYmIyZjhlMDU0NjZmYWMwNTU3NDFmMzVkMyI=).
    - Urgently consult vascular [surgery](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/7I04Vh#IlphNjhlMDEzMTM0MjM1NzIyODNjZTQxODA4N2YzNTZjYiI=) if bleeding is heavy, persists, or recurs.

### **Dialysis disequilibrium syndrome [3][17]**

- **Definition**: the development of acute [cerebral edema](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/HL0K_g#IlphY2ExYjk4NzgyZjRmMjYzMWMwNmYwNDE0YTU5ZWU3YiI=) secondary to the rapid extraction of osmotically active substances (e.g., [urea](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/FK0gRS#IlowNGQ1MzVlMDhhZTIxYzExMjYyODQ4NmUwMzY2NjRhMSI=), NaCl) from the blood
- [**Risk factors**](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/ps0LDh#IlpiYzBkMGZmMTUzYmM1YzdhMjc1ODk4MTVkMGM4NDJmZCI=)
    
    [18]
    
    - First dialysis sessions
    - Extremely elevated [BUN](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/kg0mv2#Ilo5YTdjNzkxNzJmZDllMzUzNWZlYzRkOWY5MzgxZjNjNyI=)
    - [Metabolic acidosis](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/zL0rZS#IlplNmVjZDFhNjg5ZjIxYzUwZjZmMWQ3N2RjZDJiZDEwYiI=)
    - Preexisting neurological abnormalities
    - [Hyperglycemia](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/3g0SE2#Ilo0MTc2NWNmMDA5N2M3ZWE2Zjc4ODQzMGIxNGIxMGEzNSI=) or [hypernatremia](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/Oq0IAS#IlpmOTNmZGJhNWEwOGE0MjVkODUyZWE4YjlkNmMzOTIxZSI=)
- **Prevention**
    
    [17]
    
    - Use [hemofiltration](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#Ilo0Mzc4N2QyOGIwZTAzMWIyMjUxNGE2ZGI0MTY4ZDhlYiI=) over [hemodialysis](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#IlpiY2NkNjI3OTIyYzNhYzM5NzgyY2I2NzVlZDRjMWJjZCI=) in patients with [risk factors](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/ps0LDh#IlpiYzBkMGZmMTUzYmM1YzdhMjc1ODk4MTVkMGM4NDJmZCI=).
    - If [hemodialysis](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#IlpiY2NkNjI3OTIyYzNhYzM5NzgyY2I2NzVlZDRjMWJjZCI=) is performed, provide regular, slow dialysis.
    - Consider adjusting the dialysate to mitigate osmotic shifts.
        
        [17]
        
        [18]
        
- **Management**: Monitor patients for symptoms of cerebral edema and initiate [management of raised ICP](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/HL0K_g#Ilo4YzM4YThmNDA3MWI0N2M5ODY1NTg3ZDNjOGZmMDVjZiI=) if present.
    
    [17]

**Peritoneal dialysis**

### **Basic principles [2][22]**

- **Mechanism**: utilizes diffusion across the [peritoneum](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/LJ0wuS#Ilo1Y2FkZTY2OTUyYzE5N2MyOWI1NTI2NjZlMmY5NWFlZiI=) (acts as a semipermeable membrane) and ultrafiltration to remove water and solutes from the blood
- **Procedure**
    - Dialysate is instilled into the abdomen and left for a set period of time (i.e., dwell time) to allow diffusion.
    - Hypertonic dialysate  draws water across the [peritoneal](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/LJ0wuS#Ilo1Y2FkZTY2OTUyYzE5N2MyOWI1NTI2NjZlMmY5NWFlZiI=) membrane via [osmosis](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/860O5S#Ilo2NmMxMmJhYTAzYjc0YzUwODJhMTQ2M2EwMjExNDRkOCI=).
    - The effluent is removed at the end of the dwell time.
- **Administration options**: self-administered by highly adherent patients (or their carers) at home
    
    [23]
    
    - Continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis
        - Performed manually 3–5 times/day
        - No requirement to be connected to a machine
    - Automated peritoneal dialysis
        - Automated exchange cycles, typically scheduled overnight
        - Patients are connected to a machine.
- [**Peritoneal](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/LJ0wuS#Ilo1Y2FkZTY2OTUyYzE5N2MyOWI1NTI2NjZlMmY5NWFlZiI=) catheter**
    - Location: placed into the [peritoneal cavity](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/LJ0wuS#IloxMDg3ZTg5ZGFlMWRjMWExMGFiMmFlMzc0YmE1NzM1MyI=) and tunneled to an exit site
        
        [22]
        
    - Placement: [laparoscopic](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/B50zmg#IlpmMTc3MDk3NmNlOWRmYmJkMDY3YzgyMDY3YTQxYWQyMSI=), percutaneous (with or without image guidance), or open surgical approach

Because of the high risk of complications, individuals must be trained and able to demonstrate strict adherence to correct techniques before being allowed to perform home [peritoneal dialysis](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#IloxYWRlYWJhMjJmNjFhZWVjMmJiMDNmZWVlYmYxZTQ3YiI=). [2]

### **Complications of peritoneal dialysis [2][22][24]**

- Metabolic disturbances: weight gain, [hyperglycemia](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/3g0SE2#Ilo0MTc2NWNmMDA5N2M3ZWE2Zjc4ODQzMGIxNGIxMGEzNSI=)
- Infections
    - Exit site and catheter tunnel infections
    - [Peritoneal dialysis-associated peritonitis](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#IloxNGM2OTllMDIzMjhkZGQ0YjQ4ZmNiOGExOTYwNDU3MyI=)
- Protein loss: [hypoalbuminemia](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/6n0j8g#IlpjZTBhMTQzYzY4YWVjMmMxYTkwMjUzMzgwZGVkMjI5YiI=)
    
    [25]
    
- [Abdominal hernias](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/030eSf#IlplNzU0MmMwZjZjZDQ0YWE2YTg5YWUwNmY4NjdkMjVhZCI=): umbilical and [inguinal hernias](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/zh0rgf#Ilo1M2Y1YTMxYjhmYWU2NjhmNTdkN2JmNGRiZmViMzRjMyI=) are most common
    
    [26]
    
- Leakage of dialysate
- [Pleural effusion](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/F50gNg#Ilo0ZjZjYzgxNDkwZjgwNDU0NjU3MTgxMDMzYzBmMDIxNCI=) (rare)
    
    [27]
    
    [28]
    

Frequently examine the [peritoneal dialysis](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#IloxYWRlYWJhMjJmNjFhZWVjMmJiMDNmZWVlYmYxZTQ3YiI=) catheter and exit site for signs of infection (e.g., swelling, [purulent](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/qh0CUf#IlpmZGZiODJkODNlNjc1NTEzYzQ5NjE5YmM2NDZmZDIwYyI=) drainage); consider [ultrasound](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/4n03tg#Ilo0MjU0ZDcxZWE5MWJiODVhMThlZWVhYWY1ZTdhZGJlZiI=) for a more detailed assessment if infection is suspected. [24]

**Peritoneal dialysis-associated peritonitis**

### [**Risk factors](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/ps0LDh#IlpiYzBkMGZmMTUzYmM1YzdhMjc1ODk4MTVkMGM4NDJmZCI=) [29]**

- [Obesity](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/4g03v2#Ilo2ZjhlNTA0MzZlZTJkYjdiOTgyYzI5MGIyOWY5ZGRiYyI=)
- Smoking
- Depression
- [Hypoalbuminemia](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/6n0j8g#IlpjZTBhMTQzYzY4YWVjMmMxYTkwMjUzMzgwZGVkMjI5YiI=)
- [*Staphylococcus aureus*](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/Vl0GDT#IlphZjA3NGVkZmI0OTQwYmUwZWQxZDRmY2ZjNGVhMGRlZSI=) nasal carriage
- Previous exit site infection
- Inadequate [peritoneal dialysis](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#IloxYWRlYWJhMjJmNjFhZWVjMmJiMDNmZWVlYmYxZTQ3YiI=) training
- Pets in the area where [peritoneal dialysis](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/S50yjg#IloxYWRlYWJhMjJmNjFhZWVjMmJiMDNmZWVlYmYxZTQ3YiI=) is being performed
- Invasive abdominal procedures (e.g., [colonoscopy](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/T806N3#IlphNGFlYmM4YmFhMTMxYjgzNjIwMjkyZThkMDM2MzczOSI=), [cholecystectomy](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/kG0mzh#IlozNDg2ZmQ5OTFmZjVmZTQ3M2VhZjIxZjExMjA3YWJjMyI=), [hysteroscopy](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/SN0y0g#IlplMTk2ODY1YjlhYmRiMzE3ODI1OTg2M2QzODEzNGZiYSI=))

### **Clinical features [29]**

- Often asymptomatic and identified by cloudy [peritoneal](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/LJ0wuS#Ilo1Y2FkZTY2OTUyYzE5N2MyOWI1NTI2NjZlMmY5NWFlZiI=) effluent
- Clinical features of peritonitis may be present.

### **Diagnostics [29]**

- Perform a comprehensive clinical evaluation, including an examination of the catheter.
- Obtain a [peritoneal](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/LJ0wuS#Ilo1Y2FkZTY2OTUyYzE5N2MyOWI1NTI2NjZlMmY5NWFlZiI=) fluid sample.
    - Send for cell count with differential, [Gram stain](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/In0Yug#Ilo4MTRkMjUzY2UyNmU4MDA5MjI2Y2UxMmI5MDlkMTViNyI=), and cultures.
    - If the effluent is cloudy, initiate [empiric antibiotic therapy](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/mm0VTg#IlowZDkyYWYxZWFiMjM5NDY1NzIxZGEwMjU0OWM2MTg1NCI=).
- Systemic signs of infection: Initiate [diagnostic workup for sepsis](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/xm0E3g#IlphZmEyMzFmM2U3ZWFjNmVjMzU2MDM2YWVmMTRjNWM4MiI=).
- Confirm peritonitis if ≥ 2 of the following are present:
    - Clinical features of peritonitis
    - [Peritoneal](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/LJ0wuS#Ilo1Y2FkZTY2OTUyYzE5N2MyOWI1NTI2NjZlMmY5NWFlZiI=) fluid with > 100 [WBCs](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/ln0vtg#IloxOGEwMjg3MzAyM2NjZWY2ZDhlYTJiNDYxZTBiNzQ0NiI=)/mcL (typically > 50% polymorphonuclear cells)
        
        [30]
        
    - Positive [peritoneal](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/LJ0wuS#Ilo1Y2FkZTY2OTUyYzE5N2MyOWI1NTI2NjZlMmY5NWFlZiI=) fluid culture

Initiate [empiric antibiotic therapy](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/mm0VTg#IlowZDkyYWYxZWFiMjM5NDY1NzIxZGEwMjU0OWM2MTg1NCI=) for presumed peritonitis in all patients with cloudy [peritoneal](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/LJ0wuS#Ilo1Y2FkZTY2OTUyYzE5N2MyOWI1NTI2NjZlMmY5NWFlZiI=) effluent, even if they are asymptomatic. [29]

### **Management [29][31][32]**

### **Initial management**

- [**Features of sepsis**](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/xm0E3g#IlpmYzUwMDA2MzVjODJkZjgxNjkwY2NlMDRmMDE3YTljYyI=)
    - Admit the patient.
    - Initiate [sepsis management](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/xm0E3g#IlpjZmRhZDFlYTQ3NDA1OThlOTcxZGFhOTg4MDY1ZDBhZSI=), including empiric **systemic** [antibiotics](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/mm0VTg#Ilo0NjYwZjUzYjJiMGM4NWFhM2Y5YWIwMWJkZjNmMTRmMyI=).
        
        [29]
        
- **No systemic signs of infection (common**)
    - Consult nephrology and infectious diseases; most cases can be managed in an outpatient setting
    - Initiate broad-spectrum empiric **intraperitoneal** [antibiotics](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/mm0VTg#Ilo0NjYwZjUzYjJiMGM4NWFhM2Y5YWIwMWJkZjNmMTRmMyI=).
        
        [29]
        
- [**Empiric antibiotic](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/mm0VTg#IlowZDkyYWYxZWFiMjM5NDY1NzIxZGEwMjU0OWM2MTg1NCI=) regimens**
    - Adjust the dose based on renal function per hospital protocol.
    - Recommended regimens include:
        
        [29]

        - [Vancomycin](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/mm0VTg#Ilo2YTZkYzZlNTNjOGE0ODk1ZjRjM2I1YjJmZjJhMjBiMiI=) OR [first-generation cephalosporin](https://next.amboss.com/us/article/mm0VTg#Ilo1NjkzYTkwNTIyZmI2ZmY4MTRkYjI4MTdlOGQzZmJmYyI=) (gram-positive cover)

### **Ongoing management**

- Addressing modifiable risk factors

Trasplante

### Descripción General

- **Número de procedimientos (2019, EE.UU.)**: 23,401
- **Indicación**: Pacientes con enfermedad renal terminal (ERC 5)

### Contraindicaciones

- **Absolutas**:
    - Anatomía vascular inadecuada
    - Bypass aortobifemoral o un injerto de stent aortoilíaco que se extiende a ambas arterias ilíacas externas
    - Calcificación circunferencial de los vasos ilíacos
    - Trombosis de la vena ilíaca y la vena cava inferior
    - Infección activa (e.g., tuberculosis, infecciones fúngicas invasivas, osteomielitis)
    - Malignidad en los últimos 2 años
    - IMC ≥ 50 kg/m²
    - Uso activo de alcohol o sustancias (excepto tabaco)
    - Falta de apoyo social adecuado (e.g., paciente en un hogar de ancianos, paciente sin hogar)
- **Relativas**:
    - Edad < 1 año o > 75 años
    - Enfermedades del tracto urinario inferior
- **Específicas para donantes vivos**:
    - Embarazo
    - Enfermedades psiquiátricas o problemas psicosociales
    - Condiciones que potencialmente pueden dañar los riñones (e.g., proteinuria > 300 mg/día, hipertensión no controlada, diabetes mellitus)

### Técnica

- **Riñón preferido**: Riñón izquierdo (vena renal más larga)
- **Sitio de trasplante**: Fosa ilíaca
    - Palpación, biopsia y evaluación por ultrasonido más fáciles
    - Anastomosis vascular con arterias inguinales más sencilla
    - Menor distancia entre uréter y vejiga

### Complicaciones del Trasplante Renal

- **En cualquier momento post-trasplante**:
    - Infecciones post-trasplante
    - Rechazo del injerto (e.g., rechazo agudo)
    - Efectos adversos de inmunosupresores (e.g., nefrotoxicidad inducida por calcineurinas)
    - Recurrencia de la enfermedad primaria
- **Postoperatorias agudas (< 1 semana)**:
    - Necrosis tubular aguda
    - Fuga urinaria
        - **Características clínicas**: aumento de la salida del drenaje postoperatorio, dolor e hinchazón del injerto, fiebre, urinoma
        - **Diagnóstico**: imagen abdominal, análisis bioquímico del fluido de drenaje
    - Trombosis de la vena renal (raro y grave)
        - **Características clínicas**: oliguria, hematuria, dolor/hinchazón del injerto
        - **Diagnóstico**: ultrasonido Doppler
        - **Tratamiento**: intervención quirúrgica urgente (e.g., trombectomía, nefrectomía)
- **Tempranas (1–12 semanas)**:
    - Obstrucción del tracto urinario (aguda)
        - **Etiología**: compresión extraluminal (e.g., hematoma), edema, trombo, torsión de la anastomosis
        - **Características clínicas**: cólico renal, hematuria, oliguria
    - Linfoceles
        - **Características clínicas**: a menudo asintomático o síntomas inespecíficos
        - **Diagnóstico**: análisis bioquímico y microscopía de la colección de fluidos
    - Trombosis de la arteria renal (raro y grave)
- **Tardías (> 12 semanas)**:
    - Estenosis de la arteria renal: complicación vascular más común (inicio ∼ 6 meses post-trasplante)
        - **Etiología**: falla de la anastomosis, ateroma, mediada por el sistema inmunológico
        - **Características clínicas**: disfunción renal, hipertensión
        - **Diagnóstico**: ultrasonido, angiografía por TC o RM
    - Obstrucción del tracto urinario (subaguda o crónica)
        - **Etiología**: fibrosis del uréter
        - **Características clínicas**: hipertensión; a menudo asintomático
    - Malignidad post-trasplante (e.g., carcinoma de células renales)

### Cuidado Post-trasplante

- **Inmediato (dentro de las 24 horas)**:
    - Puede ser necesaria la diálisis para la función retardada del injerto
- **Manejo a largo plazo**:
    - Monitoreo seriado de pruebas de función renal (e.g., creatinina sérica, proteína urinaria)
    - Terapia inmunosupresora post-trasplante
    - Prevención de infecciones post-trasplante

### Algoritmo Diagnóstico para Disfunción Renal

1. Considerar causas prerrenales (e.g., deshidratación, estenosis de la arteria renal).
2. Medir la relación BUN, proteína urinaria y hematuria.
3. Ajustar niveles de anticalcineurinas si es necesario.
4. Realizar ultrasonido renal y de vejiga para descartar obstrucción.
5. Realizar biopsia renal si está indicado.

### Pronóstico

- **Duración de la función del injerto**: ∼ 14 años (más larga con donante vivo)
- **Tasas de supervivencia**:
    - **1 año**: Injerto cadavérico 88%, Donante vivo 94%
    - **2 años**: Injerto cadavérico 81%, Donante vivo 93%
    - **5 años**: Injerto cadavérico 71%, Donante vivo 84%

El trasplante renal tiene un mejor pronóstico que la diálisis en la enfermedad renal terminal.
