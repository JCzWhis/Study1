## **Abstract**

### **Background**

The C5a receptor inhibitor avacopan is being studied for the treatment of antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA)–associated vasculitis.

### **Methods**

In this randomized, controlled trial, we assigned patients with ANCA-associated vasculitis in a 1:1 ratio to receive oral avacopan at a dose of 30 mg twice daily or oral prednisone on a tapering schedule. All the patients received either cyclophosphamide (followed by azathioprine) or rituximab. The first primary end point was remission, defined as a Birmingham Vasculitis Activity Score (BVAS) of 0 (on a scale from 0 to 63, with higher scores indicating greater disease activity) at week 26 and no glucocorticoid use in the previous 4 weeks. The second primary end point was sustained remission, defined as remission at both weeks 26 and 52. Both end points were tested for noninferiority (by a margin of 20 percentage points) and for superiority.

*Download a PDF of the [Research Summary](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2023386#ap0).*

![](https://www.nejm.org/cms/10.1056/NEJMoa2023386/asset/f1989641-c659-4521-9760-b6bf68d7db29/assets/images/large/nejmoa2023386_f0.jpg)

### **Results**

A total of 331 patients underwent randomization; 166 were assigned to receive avacopan, and 165 were assigned to receive prednisone. The mean BVAS at baseline was 16 in both groups. Remission at week 26 (the first primary end point) was observed in 120 of 166 patients (72.3%) receiving avacopan and in 115 of 164 patients (70.1%) receiving prednisone (estimated common difference, 3.4 percentage points; 95% confidence interval [CI], −6.0 to 12.8; P<0.001 for noninferiority; P=0.24 for superiority). Sustained remission at week 52 (the second primary end point) was observed in 109 of 166 patients (65.7%) receiving avacopan and in 90 of 164 patients (54.9%) receiving prednisone (estimated common difference, 12.5 percentage points; 95% CI, 2.6 to 22.3; P<0.001 for noninferiority; P=0.007 for superiority). Serious adverse events (excluding worsening vasculitis) occurred in 37.3% of the patients receiving avacopan and in 39.0% of those receiving prednisone.

### **Conclusions**

In this trial involving patients with ANCA-associated vasculitis, avacopan was noninferior but not superior to prednisone taper with respect to remission at week 26 and was superior to prednisone taper with respect to sustained remission at week 52. All the patients received cyclophosphamide or rituximab. The safety and clinical effects of avacopan beyond 52 weeks were not addressed in the trial. (Funded by ChemoCentryx; ADVOCATE ClinicalTrials.gov number, [NCT02994927](http://clinicaltrials.gov/show/NCT02994927).)

**Methods:** RITAZAREM was an international randomised controlled, open-label, superiority trial that recruited 188 patients at the time of an AAV relapse from 29 centres in seven countries between April 2013 and November 2016. All patients received rituximab and glucocorticoids to reinduce remission. Patients achieving remission by 4 months were randomised to receive rituximab intravenously (1000 mg every 4 months, through month 20) (85 patients) or azathioprine (2 mg/kg/day, tapered after month 24) (85 patients) and followed for a minimum of 36 months. The primary outcome was time to disease relapse (either major or minor relapse).

**Results:** Rituximab was superior to azathioprine in preventing relapse: HR 0.41; 95% CI 0.27 to 0.61, p<0.001. 19/85 (22%) patients in the rituximab group and 31/85 (36%) in the azathioprine group experienced at least one serious adverse event during the treatment period. There were no differences in rates of hypogammaglobulinaemia or infection between groups.

**Conclusions:** Following induction of remission with rituximab, fixed-interval, repeat-dose rituximab was superior to azathioprine for preventing disease relapse in patients with AAV with a prior history of relapse.

Studies on rituximab treatment for remission maintenance
and the evolution of the COVID-19 pandemic solidified
concerns about continuous B-cell depletion, which has the
potential to induce secondary immunodeficiency, decrease

defined as a serum immunoglobulin

of remission (per 10 mg increase of prednisone). The
concern of hypogammaglobulinaemia

Glucocorticoid toxicity
In the treatment of ANCA-associated vasculitis, the use of
glucocorticoids has been considered unavoidable to
control disease. Clinical trials published in the past 4 years
have evaluated the safety and efficacy of novel and
established agents to reduce cumulative exposure to
glucocorticoids (tables 4, 5).73,77,81 For example, the
PEXIVAS reduced-dose regimen was non-inferior to a
standard-dose regimen in terms of efficacy, substantially
decreased the cumulative glucocorticoid dose needed to
control the disease, and reduced the risk of serious
infections.81 All three major guidelines or
recommendations acknowledge the reduced-dose
regimen as the new standard of care.75 The ADVOCATE
trial replaced a standard prednisone taper with avacopan
for ANCA-associated vasculitis and marked an important
step forward for vasculitis trials, because it used a
standardised instrument to measure change in
glucocorticoid toxicity.77,116 The avacopan group had lower
Glucocorticoid Toxicity Index (GTI) scores at both
13 weeks and 26 weeks after treatment initiation.117 In
addition, avacopan was superior to the standard of care in
reducing glucocorticoid toxicity at several GTI thresholds,
including the minimum clinically important difference of
10 points.116
In practice settings, high glucocorticoid doses tend to
be maintained for longer periods of time and patients are
often left on maintenance glucocorticoid regimens.
Thus, real-world patients are more likely to receive
substantially more glucocorticoids than are patients in
trials. Although patients who reached the primary
endpoint discontinued their prednisone no later than
21 weeks into the ADVOCATE trial,77 glucocorticoid
toxicity continued to increase in both the avacopan group
and the prednisone group at both GTI timepoints. More
than 90% of all patients in the trial had glucocorticoid
toxicity at both week 13 and week 26. These findings
underscore the need for strategies that reduce the
duration of glucocorticoid courses further, minimising
glucocorticoid use but still exerting and maintaining
disease control.

Novel treatment strategies over the next few years will
probably include tissue-adjacent B-cell subsets with
traditional anti-CD20 approaches, such as obinutuzumab
or newer monoclonal antibodies targeting CD19. Research
into anti-CD19 directed CAR-T therapies for patients with
ANCA-associated vasculitis with the greatest unmet
medical need is now increasing rapidly. Other B celltargeted
therapies aimed at immunomodulation rather
than depletion, and further efforts to inhibit relevant
components of complement pathways are also promising
potential strategies. Competitive interference with the
neonatal Fc receptor as a way of swiftly reducing ANCA
titres to facilitate disease control is another appealing
approach. There could also be improvement in both
induction and remission, with more effective and quicker
means of suppressing disease flares early, as well as
approaches to remission maintenance that do not involve
continuous B-cell depletion or long-term glucocorticoid
use.
Over the next 10 years, the likelihood of continued
incremental progress for patients with ANCA-associated
vasculitis is high. Substantial leaps forward in the ability
to diagnose, treat, and monitor ANCA-associated
vasculitis, however, will require breakthroughs in 

understanding disease biology. Efforts to illuminate the
basic mechanisms of ANCA-associated vasculitis are
challenged by collecting sufficient numbers of biological
samples from well characterised patients, and getting
those samples into laboratories most capable of addressing
translational questions specific to ANCA-associated
vasculitis.

*Mepolizumab es eficaz en la granulomatosis eosinofílica con poliangitis (GEPA) debido a su capacidad para inhibir la interleucina-5 (IL-5), una citoquina clave en la producción y activación de eosinófilos, que juegan un papel central en la fisiopatología de la GEPA. Esta enfermedad está caracterizada por una infiltración eosinofílica significativa en los tejidos y un alto recuento de eosinófilos en sangre.*

*Al bloquear la IL-5, mepolizumab reduce la diferenciación, proliferación y activación de los eosinófilos, disminuyendo la inflamación eosinofílica que contribuye al daño orgánico en la GEPA. Esto se traduce en una mejoría en la sintomatología, reducción de exacerbaciones y menor necesidad de corticosteroides, mejorando así el control de la enfermedad sin los efectos adversos asociados a la inmunosupresión intensiva.*

    *El uso de RTX según CD19+, no tenía efecto (MAINRITSAN-2)*
    
    *Según el MAINRITSAN-3 el uso de RTX en GPA o MPA produce en 21% de los casos en 36 meses hipogammaglobilinemia (IgG <5 g/dl)*
    
    *El cotrimoxazol baja las infecciones severas, principalmente P. jirovecii, pero también otras infecciones en general, el beneficio es mayor si usan 30 o más de prednisona por 4 o más semanas*
    
    *Alternativas a Cotrimoxazol: Dapsona, Atovaquona, Pentamidina en aerosol*
    

**Tratamiento en situaciones especiales**

- Nasal – paranasal y otitis media
    - Tratamiento sistémico
    - Tratamiento tópico nasal con lavados diarios con suero y lubricante
    - Control con ORL
- Ocular
    - Tratamiento como enfermedad generalizada

**Manejo en diálisis o trasplante renal**

Pronóstico renal se mantiene suboptimo, 20-25% hacen falla renal.

Recurrencias son bajas, considerar sacar inmunosupresores si lleva 6 meses en remisión extra-renal, especialmente en MPO. 

En caso de trasplante, la recurrencia es baja, se recomienda estar en remisión 12 meses antes. 

Si ya esta en falla renal terminal no es necesaria la mantención. 

### Uso de plasmaféresis en ANCA

Debatido:

- A favor:
    - Beneficios en función renal, reduce el riesgo de insuficiencia renal terminal (baja necesidad de diálisis 40%), dependiendo del daño inicial, se benefician más los con enfermedad severa.
    - Considerarlo en pacientes graves o con ANCA+Anti-MBG
    - Si bien PEXIVAS no mostró beneficio en hemorragia alveolar, se considera que no tiene poder estadístico suficiente
- Argumentos en contra
    - Riesgo de infecciones graves, RR 1,27
    - No ha demostrado reducir la mortalidad en hemorragia alveolar grave (PEXIVAS)
        - No redujo tiempo de VM ni gravedad
    - No mejora supervivencia global ni calidad de vida
    - No reduce la tasa de recaídas de vasculitis
    - Beneficio renal tiende a disminuir con el tiempo
    - Caro, invasivo

***Lo más discutido es por la hemorragia pulmonar,*** PEXIVAS dice que no sirve
