# Anemia hemolítica autoinmune

**AHA:** grupos heterogéneo de anemias por autoanticuerpos contra las membranas de los GR.

Clasificados por tipo de anticuerpos y su respuesta a la temperatura:

- AHA por anticuerpos calientes 65%
- AHA por anticuerpos fríos 29%
- Hemoglobinuria fría paroxística 1%
- AHA mixta 5%

Otra clasificación

- Idiopática o primaria 50%
- Secundaria 50% → autoinmune, linfoproliferativo, drogas (ATB, antineoplásicos)

### **Anemia hemolítica autoinmune por anticuerpos calientes**

La más común (5-10 casos por millón al año)

Generalmente **IgG policlonal** (algunos IgM e IgA) que reacciones a antígenos de membrana de GR (generalmente epítopos Rh)

Hemólisis es **extravascular,** en bazo por los macrófagos.

Anticuerpos  (clásica) →  Complemento → MAC → intravascular (es lo menos)

 Nombre de anticuerpos calientes porque tienen máxima afinidad a los 37°C.

50% son primario o idiopáticos. El resto puede ser asociado a LES, inmunodeficiencia común variable, linfoproliferativos (LMC o linfoma T), polimorfismos de CTLA4, COVID severo

The pathogenesis of warm AIHA is complex ([Figure 1](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#f1)). The mononuclear phagocytic system in the spleen plays a major role in the breakdown of opsonized erythrocytes (extravascular hemolysis).[**11,12**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r11) The complement system, activated by the classical pathway, is involved in approximately half the cases.[**6,13**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r6) Most of the complement-mediated erythrocyte destruction occurs through phagocytosis of complement fragment 3b (C3b)–coated cells (extravascular hemolysis). To a lesser extent, cleavage of C5 and activation of the terminal complement cascade may occur, resulting in formation of the membrane attack complex and intravascular hemolysis. The pathogenesis of this disorder also involves the T-lymphocyte system.[**14-16**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r14) The signal substance cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen 4 (CTLA-4) activates regulatory T cells, which are critical for immune tolerance.[**17**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r17) Polymorphism of the *CTLA-4* gene can confer a predisposition to immunologic disorders, including autoimmune cytopenias.[**15**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r15) The programmed cell death 1 (PD-1) signal pathway is another checkpoint for immune tolerance, and pharmacologic inhibition of PD-1 carries an increased risk of autoimmune disease.[**18**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r18)

**Figure 1***Pathogenesis of Warm Antibody–Mediated and Cold Antibody–Mediated Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia.*

In nearly 50% of cases of warm AIHA, no underlying or associated disorder can be identified, and the hemolytic disease is classified as primary.[**1,6**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1) Slightly more than 50% of the cases are secondary to immunologic or lymphoproliferative disorders (e.g., chronic lymphocytic leukemia, systemic lupus erythematosus, or common variable immunodeficiency).[**5,6,14,19**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r5) Occasionally, a lymphoproliferative disease develops several years after the diagnosis of AIHA.[**16**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r16) Recently, several cases have been described in patients with severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) infection.[**20**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r20) The Evans syndrome, characterized by the simultaneous or sequential combination of AIHA and immune thrombocytopenia, is often associated with more severe anemia and a worse prognosis than primary warm AIHA.[**6,21,22**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r6)

### Anemia hemolítica autoinmune por anticuerpos fríos

Incidencia de 0,45 a 1,9 casos por millón al año

Causada por **IgM monoclonal** contra antígeno i de los eritrocitos. Máxima afinidad entre 0 y 4°C

Como la IgM es pentamérica, es capaz de tomar varios GR, lo que hace que retarde el paso por capilares, el intercambio gaseoso producirá Raynaud y acrocianosis (40-90%), lo que es característico de esta condición, la gangrena es rara. Además es un activador más potente del complemento.

Está relacionado a linfoproliferativos como LNH de bajo grado, linfoma de zona marginal o linfoplasmocitico. 

Hemólisis depende del complemento, vía clásica, se opsoniza eritrocito con C3b y se fagocita en el hígado.

## **Diagnosis**

A diagnostic algorithm is shown in [Figure 3](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#f3), and [Table 2](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#t2) provides a summary of diagnostic procedures. Hemolytic anemia is diagnosed on the basis of the hemoglobin level and serum levels of lactate dehydrogenase, unconjugated bilirubin, and haptoglobin.[**1,4,47**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1) Absolute reticulocytosis supports the presence of hemolysis, but the reticulocyte count may be normal or low,[**1,47**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1) probably because of autoantibody activity against erythrocyte precursors or a coexisting bone marrow disorder.

Autoimmune pathogenesis is detected by means of the direct antiglobulin test (DAT, also termed Coombs’ test). A positive test shows immunoglobulin, complement, or both on the erythrocyte surface. A positive polyspecific (simple) DAT must be followed by a monospecific (extended) DAT, which will identify the immunoglobulin class (or classes) or complement protein bound to the red cell. Complement components, most often C3d, are detected in nearly half the cases of warm AIHA because of complement activation. If warm-reactive IgM is involved, the DAT may be positive or negative for IgM because IgM may have detached from the cell before it can be detected in the laboratory. IgM is a potent complement activator and will leave the erythrocytes coated with C3. Therefore, a monospecific DAT that is positive for C3d and negative for IgG indicates IgM involvement.[**9**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r9)

The DAT is negative in 3 to 10% of patients with AIHA, depending on the DAT technique used.[**1,47**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1) AIHA with a negative DAT remains a difficult diagnosis of exclusion. A false negative result can be due to a low density of immunoglobulin, complement, or both on the cell surface. A rare cause of false negative results is warm AIHA with IgA as the only autoantibody class, since most polyspecific reagents do not contain anti-IgA and thus cannot detect IgA. This problem can be overcome by performing a monospecific DAT even when the polyspecific test is negative. More sensitive DAT methods (microcolumn, solid phase, cold washes, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, flow cytometry, dual DAT, and mitogen-stimulated DAT) in reference laboratories may be useful. Nonimmune causes of hemolysis (congenital, toxic, or mechanical causes, as well as drugs and paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria) should be ruled out ([Figure 3](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#f3)).[**1,4,47**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1)

In cold agglutinin disease and the cold agglutinin syndrome, the DAT is positive for C3d by definition and is usually negative for immunoglobulins but is weakly positive for IgG in up to 20% of cases.[**27,28**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r27) This result is not sufficient for establishing the diagnosis. The cold agglutinin titer must be assessed; it will be at least 64 (an integer that represents the activity of the antibody and the inverse of the dilution [1:64]) and usually much higher.[**7,25,27**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r7) Specific precautions are required for sampling and handling blood specimens, as indicated in [Table 2](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#t2).[**1,7**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1)

Paroxysmal cold hemoglobinuria should be suspected in children with acute hemolytic anemia and should be confirmed or ruled out with the Donath–Landsteiner test.[**1,37**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1) In a positive test, binding of the antibody to red cells occurs at a low temperature, followed by complement-mediated hemolysis on subsequent warming. Cold agglutinins are not present in this disease, and the DAT is frequently positive for C3 and negative for IgG.

Once warm AIHA has been diagnosed, an assessment for causes of secondary AIHA should be undertaken.[**1,5**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1) This includes a careful review of the medical history, a thorough clinical examination, assessment of serologic markers of autoimmune diseases, and relevant viral serologic tests. Whole-body computed tomography and flow cytometry in peripheral blood should be considered.[**1**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1) Serum protein electrophoresis and quantification of immunoglobulin classes must be performed for all types of AIHA.[**7,15,27**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r7) In cold agglutinin disease, the lymphoproliferative bone marrow disorder should be identified by biopsy and flow cytometry, although in a minority of cases, it will not be detected or interpretation of the results will be difficult.[**27,29**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r27) The relevant clonal bone marrow changes are most frequently identified in specialized pathology laboratories that are familiar with this entity.[**27**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r27) Even in patients with warm AIHA, bone marrow examinations should be performed for a wide range of indications, at least in case of therapy failure.[**1**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1)

Cold agglutinins are sometimes incidentally detected in blood samples obtained for unrelated indications.[**48**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r48) In these cases, the patients often have a polyclonal, low-titer, low-thermal-amplitude cold agglutinin without hemolysis or clinical symptoms, and they do not have cold agglutinin disease or syndrome. Likewise, persons with incidental positive DAT results and no signs of hemolysis do not have AIHA.

### **Tratamiento**

***AHAI por anticuerpos calientes primaria***

**Prednisona** 1 mg/Kg como primera línea, 2-3 semanas, luego bajar. Responde 80% inicial. Remisión sostenida al año: 30-40%.

**Rituximab,** asociado a corticoides aumenta la respuesta a largo plazo, se recomienda en anemia <8 g/dl, mediadas por IgA, mixtas, síndrome de Evans. O si no responden a corticoides, tiene respuesta de 70 a 80%, tiempo de 3 a 6 semanas. 30% pueden tener recurrencia a 3 años. Dosis 375 mg/m2 semanas por 4 ciclos o  1 g por 2 ciclos cada 14 días. 

Esplenectomía si no responden a RTX. Respuesta 70%. Riesgos de infección y trombosis.

Otras opciones: AZA, CYC, CsA, MMF, Bortezomib. - Tienen pocos datos.

Emergencias: Metilprednisolona, Gammaglobulina EV, recambio plasmático, esplenectomía. Rescate, con poca evidencia: bortezomib + Dexa, TMO alogénico, Alemtuzumab.

AHAI por anticuerpos calientes secundaria se trata similar

***AHAI por anticuerpos fríos***

No corticoides porque la respuesta es baja o se necesitan dosis muy altas.

Si la anemia es leve, monitorizar.

Protección térmica.

Más usada: Monoterapia con Rituximab 375 mg/m2 cada semana. Respuesta 45-60%, pero raro que sea completa. Puede tener recurrencia a 12 - 15 meses.

Treatment with rituximab plus bendamustine, studied in a prospective trial, resulted in overall and complete response rates of 71% and 40%, respectively, which increased to 78% and 53% during long-term follow-up.[**27,67**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r27) The median time to a response was 1.9 months (upper end of the range, 12 months), and the time to the best response was up to 30 months. The deepening of responses with time and the occurrence of very late responses are probably related to long-lived plasma cells.[**27,29,68,69**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r27) The estimated median response duration was more than 88 months. Temporary grade 3 or 4 neutropenia (neutrophil count, <0.5×109 per liter) was observed in 33% of the patients, but only 11% had infection, with or without neutropenia.[**67**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r67) The frequency of late-occurring cancers did not exceed that reported in untreated patients.[**27**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r27)

In a small, prospective study of bortezomib administered as a single cycle of monotherapy, responses were observed in 32% of the participants.[**70**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r70) In theory, this proportion might be increased by extending the duration of treatment or using bortezomib-based combination therapy. In a retrospective study of therapy with ibrutinib, a Bruton’s tyrosine kinase inhibitor, responses were observed in all 10 study participants.[**71**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r71) This finding will have to be confirmed in a larger series or a prospective trial.

Complement inhibition is a promising new therapeutic approach but cannot be expected to relieve the circulatory symptoms, which are not complement-mediated.[**7,13,26,34**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r7) In a study of therapy with sutimlimab, a monoclonal antibody against complement protein C1s, patients had a rapid response and normalization of hemolysis, and the drug was safe.[**72**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r72) Sutimlimab will probably be available outside clinical trials in the near future. Favorable results have also been reported with pegcetacoplan, a pegylated peptide that inhibits C3.[**73**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r73) Unlike most B-cell–directed therapies, which are given for brief intervals, complement inhibition will probably have to be continued indefinitely to maintain its effect.[**7,13**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r7) This approach will provide a therapeutic option for patients in whom B-cell–directed therapy has failed or is contraindicated. The much shorter time to a response may be particularly helpful in patients with severe anemia and those in acute crisis.

### **Other AIHA Types**

In secondary cold agglutinin syndrome, the only established therapy is treatment of the underlying disease when possible. Although infection-associated cold agglutinin syndrome resolves some weeks after the infection has been eliminated, anemia can be profound, and therapy is largely ineffective.[**23**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r23) A response to complement inhibition has been described in single cases,[**26,35**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r26) but no systematic study has been reported.

Childhood paroxysmal cold hemoglobinuria is self-remitting, but anemia can be severe and occasionally life-threatening.[**1,37**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1) Immediate improvement was recently reported after a single dose of the complement C5 inhibitor eculizumab in a child with the postviral form of the disorder.[**37**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r37)

Mixed AIHA is generally characterized by a severe onset and a relapsing or refractory course. Glucocorticoids should be given in high doses, and rituximab should be considered early. If cold agglutinin symptoms are prominent, treatments for cold agglutinin disease are advisable. Splenectomy is discouraged.[**1,6**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1)

In patients with drug-induced hemolytic anemia, the suspected medication is discontinued.[**5,39,40**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r5) In the drug-independent subtype, recovery can be delayed for weeks after the causative medication has been stopped. The benefit of adding glucocorticoids is unproven, but this approach is frequently implemented.[**5,39**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r5)

## **Supportive Measures**

### **Transfusion**

Transfusion in patients with warm AIHA requires specific precautions.[**1,4,74**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1) Pretransfusion screening for irregular antibodies will be positive, and cross-matching of type-identical blood will show incompatibility. Indications should be restrictive, but transfusion must not be withheld in cases of life-threatening anemia.[**53**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r53) If time permits, extended phenotyping should be performed with respect to Rh subgroups and Kell, MNS, Kidd, S/s, and Duffy antigens.[**1,53**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1) Genotyping can be considered but will often be too time-consuming. Transfusion should start with a bedside in vivo compatibility test: rapid infusion of 20 ml of erythrocyte concentrate, observation for 20 minutes, and if no reaction is observed, further infusion at the usual rate.[**1**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1)

In patients with cold agglutinin disease, precautions are different. It is often possible to find compatible erythrocytes by cross-matching at 37°C, but in vivo cooling during transfusion can cause agglutination and hemolysis of patient as well as donor erythrocytes. The patient and the extremity chosen for infusion should be kept warm, and most authors recommend the use of a blood warmer during infusion.[**1,4,62**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1)

### **Prophylaxis against Thrombosis**

The role of prophylactic anticoagulation in patients with AIHA has not been established. In patients with acute exacerbations, hospitalized patients with marked hemolysis, and those who have undergone splenectomy or have additional risk factors, we recommend administration of low-molecular-weight heparin in prophylactic doses or a direct oral anticoagulant.[**1,4,8,62**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r1)

### **Other Supportive Measures**

Erythropoietin and its analogues have been shown to increase hemoglobin levels in patients with warm AIHA, as well as in those with cold agglutinin disease, and can be used as a supportive strategy.[**75,76**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r75) Plasma exchange (with the use of albumin instead of plasma, to avoid complement supplementation) has been successful in single cases of both warm and cold AIHA, although systematic studies have not been reported.[**4,77**](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2033982#core-r4) Plasma exchange can be considered in critical situations, but the effect is short-lived, and specific therapy should be initiated concomitantly.
